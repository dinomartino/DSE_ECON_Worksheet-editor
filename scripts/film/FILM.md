# Econ Worksheet — product film bible

The single source of truth for the Apple-style product film. Every agent building part
of the film reads this whole file first. Where it is silent, use taste and keep to its
principles; where your code must disagree with it, say so in your final report.
To change the finished film, follow the `econ-film` skill (`.claude/skills/econ-film/`).
The 9:16 Reels cut (same timeline, portrait format) is specified in [`FILM-9x16.md`](./FILM-9x16.md).

## 1. What we are making

A ~94-second cinematic product film for **Econ Worksheet** (the HKDSE Economics worksheet
editor in this repo), in the style of an Apple product introduction: dark cinematic 3D
moments alternating with bright, warm product moments; huge confident typography; real
product footage floating in 3D space; buttery 60 fps motion; every cut and every key
motion landing on the beat of an original score.

The film must feel **technical, professional, emotional**, and above all **smooth**:
smoothness of motion, of transitions, of camera, of timing. Smoothness is the headline
requirement from the user — treat any stutter, jump, pop, or mistimed cut as a bug.

Deliverable: `demo-media/film/econ-worksheet-film.mp4` — 1920×1080, 60 fps CFR, H.264
High, yuv420p, BT.709 tagged, AAC 256 kbps 48 kHz stereo, `+faststart`. Plus poster,
contact sheet, score stems and a README (see §9).

Everything is generated by code in this repo (`scripts/film/`), re-runnable with
`npm run film`. No stock footage, no stock music: the score is synthesised (§7), the 3D
is Three.js (§5), the product footage is the real app driven by Playwright (§6). This
keeps it license-clean and lets the music be composed *to the picture*.

## 2. Product truths (only claim what is true)

- Runs in the browser; no account, no server. Also a desktop app for macOS and Windows.
- Bilingual: English, Traditional Chinese (Hong Kong usage), or both (toolbar EN / 中文 / EN+中).
- The preview *is* the editor: double-click the printed page and type.
- MCQs, structured questions with (a)/(b) parts, marks labels, dotted answer lines.
- Numbering and marks are derived automatically; a paper summary counts MCQs, marks, minutes, pages.
- Student / Teacher toggle: the teacher copy shows answers in red. Marking schemes.
- Diagram canvas: draw demand/supply curves, equilibria, shift a copy (e.g. a tax),
  shade welfare areas (tax revenue, DWL…) from a Shade menu, labels in both languages;
  dozens of templates by topic. It is the killer feature — Word is where teachers fight
  diagrams today (user feedback: *show drawing from scratch; templates get one line*).
- Mock papers: Paper 1 MCQ with exam cover, Paper 2 question-answer booklet with cover,
  sections A–C, page frame; LQ worksheets with dotted answer space; versions A/B/C.
- Export: a native Microsoft Word `.docx` (live numbering, real styles, fully
  editable), PDF, answer key, and formats for Kahoot, Blooket, ZipGrade, key CSV.
- Name on screen: **Econ Worksheet**. Brand mark: an equilibrium cross — two cream
  strokes (supply and demand) crossing on an L-shaped axis, a blue dot where they meet,
  on a warm dark rounded tile. Vector: `design/icons/13-equilibrium-axes.svg`
  (tile `#3A342E` rx 114/512, strokes `#FCFAF6`, axis opacity .34, dot `#1E7FD4`).

Never show a third-party logo (Microsoft, Apple, Kahoot…). Naming a format in text is fine.
No fake UI: any product pixel on screen comes from the real app, the real export, or a
clearly generic frame we draw (window chrome, a generic laptop).

[boundary − d/2, boundary + d/2]; scenes must render sensibly for t slightly < 0 and
slightly > their duration.

## 4. Look, type and motion

### Worlds
- **night**: background `#000` with a faint warm radial lift (`#15120F` at centre, ~35%
  radius) — never flat grey. Ink `#F5F5F7`, secondary `#86868B`. Lighting: dark studio,
  one soft key, a cool rim, the accent `#1E7FD4` as the only saturated colour (glow
  `#4AA3FF`). Glossy black floor with a blurred reflection where objects stand.
- **day**: warm cream, from the app's own palette: background vertical gradient
  `#F5F1EA` → `#E8E1D5`, soft contact shadows (warm, `rgba(58,52,46,.18)`), ink
  `#1D1D1F`, secondary `#6E6E73`, accent `#0D77C9`. White paper is the brightest object
  on screen — only paper and screens are pure white.

### Typography (DOM overlay, crisp)
- Headlines: `-apple-system, "SF Pro Display", system-ui`, weight 600, 104–128 px at
  1080p, `letter-spacing: -0.035em`, `line-height: 1.04`. At most ~7 words. One headline
  on screen at a time.
- Sub-lines: SF Pro Display 500, 34–40 px, secondary colour, `letter-spacing: -0.01em`.
- Chinese: `"PingFang HK", "PingFang TC"`, weight 500–600, `letter-spacing: 0.02em`,
  set ~0.42× the headline size under the English, 70% opacity of the ink. Always
  Traditional Chinese, Hong Kong usage.
- Eyebrow (rare): SF Pro Text 600, 24 px, accent colour.
- A single key word may carry a gradient (`#1E7FD4 → #6CC4FF`, 100°) — at most twice in the film.
- Reveal: per word, rising 0.35em from a mask with blur 10 px → 0 and opacity 0 → 1,
  720 ms, stagger 60 ms, `expoOut`. Exit: 280 ms, blur 0 → 8 px, opacity → 0, rise
  0.12em, `quintIn`. Reading time on screen ≥ 1.0 s + 0.3 s per word.
- Title-safe: 96 px side margins, 72 px top/bottom. Never set type over busy UI; use
  negative space or a blurred backdrop.
- Copy: every on-screen string lives in `COPY` (timeline.mjs); `"\n"` forces a line
  break, an array is separate blocks. Scenes never split or rewrite it.
- Fit (`lib/type.js`): each block has a box, `maxWidth` (default: title-safe room at its
  x) and `maxLines` (default: its lines as written; `zhMaxLines` 1). Longer copy wraps
  (CJK per character, no line-start 。，、？！）」), then the block shrinks as one, Chinese
  included, to 80%; past that it warns. Stacked blocks read `lines`/`scale` to reflow.
  A scene whose layout has room for more lines than the copy uses says so (`maxLines`),
  so a one-line rewrite can still wrap there.

### Motion principles
1. **Nothing is ever still.** Every shot carries a slow continuous drift (1–3% dolly or
   2–4° orbit over the shot). Holds are drifts, not freezes.
2. **Ease everything.** Entrances `expoOut`/`quintOut` 600–1200 ms; exits `quintIn`
   250–450 ms; camera moves `quintInOut` or critically-damped springs. Linear motion only
   for drifts. Springs: damping ratio 0.65–0.85, at most one visible overshoot.
3. **On the beat.** Cuts on downbeats (bar or half-bar). Key motions *land* on beats
   (a curve finishing its drag, a chip popping, a headline's first word).
4. **Continuity.** Hand objects across cuts: the mark's strokes return as D and S; the
   diagram lands on the page that `marks` starts on; that page is one of the `papers`.
5. **Depth.** Hero 3D shots use depth of field (focus on the subject, bokeh behind).
   Fast camera moves get motion blur (§5.4). Never both on text.
6. **Restraint.** No lens flares, no chromatic aberration, no bounce-y cartoon easing,
   no spinning for its own sake, no more than one effect per moment. Apple, not a
   YouTube intro.
7. **Legibility.** A product frame shown for reading holds ≥ 1.5 s. UI text on a window
   is readable at 1080p (the window occupies ≥ 55% of frame width when it matters).

## 5. The stage — Three.js composition renderer (`scripts/film/stage/`)

### 5.1 Stack
- Three.js from `scripts/film/node_modules/three` (the film has its own
  `scripts/film/package.json`; the app's root dependencies are never touched). Import
  map: `three` → `/film/node_modules/three/build/three.module.js`, `three/addons/` →
  `/film/node_modules/three/examples/jsm/`.
- One WebGL canvas (1920×1080 by default; `?w=&h=&fps=&shutter=` query) plus a DOM
  overlay (`#type`) for typography, same size, above the canvas.
- `renderer.outputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`; screenshot/sheet textures are
  `SRGBColorSpace`. **UI and paper materials are unlit or calibrated so white stays
  white** (`toneMapped: false` on screens; paper lit so its face reads ≥ #F4F4F4).
  Tone mapping (AgX or ACES) only for 3D objects (tile, floor). Bloom is selective —
  the blue dot and glows only, never paper or UI.

### 5.2 Determinism
- `window.film = { ready(): Promise, fps, duration, seek(t): Promise, events(): [...] }`.
- `seek(t)` renders the frame at film time t as a **pure function of t** — no clocks, no
  accumulated state, no `Math.random()` (use a seeded hash/noise of (id, t)). Seeking
  frames out of order must give identical pixels.
- `seek` resolves only when every texture needed for t (including clip frames) is
  uploaded and the DOM is laid out (fonts loaded), after a double rAF.

### 5.3 Scene API (`stage/scenes/<id>.js`)
```js
export default {
  id: 'write',
  world: 'day',                       // default background + lighting preset
  async setup(ctx) {},                // build objects in ctx.scene, DOM in ctx.el; preload via ctx.load
  update(t, ctx) {},                  // t = seconds since the scene's start bar (may be <0 or >dur in transitions)
  events: [{ t: 0.0, kind: 'hit', strength: 1 }], // optional extra scene-relative sound cues
};
```
`ctx` gives: `THREE`, `scene`, `camera` (PerspectiveCamera the scene drives), `el`
(overlay root for this scene), `dur`, `beat` (0.5), `bar` (2.0), `W`, `H`, `world`,
`load.texture(path)`, `load.clip(name)` (§5.6), `load.json(path)`, `lib` (§5.5),
`post` (per-scene DOF/bloom settings), and `placeClip(name, {at, from, rate})` to
declare clip placements (drives UI sound events).

### 5.4 Engine features
- Scene windows and transitions from `scripts/film/timeline.mjs`; transitions
  `cut | dissolve | blurDissolve | pushThrough | whip | dipToBlack` composited from
  per-scene render targets; the DOM overlays of both scenes cross-fade with them.
- **Motion blur by temporal supersampling**: K sub-frames across a 180° shutter
  (t − ½·shutter … t + ½·shutter), averaged in a float target, for the WebGL layer.
  `shutter=5` for the final, `1` for previews.
- Post: optional DOF (bokeh) per scene, selective bloom, a very subtle vignette, and a
  **dither grain** (animated, ±1.5/255, ±2.5/255 in the shadows, seeded by frame) on the
  final output so dark gradients do not band in H.264 (the final encode keeps it, §8).
- `blurDissolve` peaks at 8 px (paper stays legible). Never dissolve between two frames
  that already match: that only defocuses the downbeat; cut.
- Lazy asset loading per active scene and disposal after, so any worker can render any
  frame without holding the whole film in memory.

### 5.5 Library (`stage/lib/`) — shared by every scene, use it instead of re-inventing
- `ease.js` — cubic-bezier, `expoOut`, `quintOut`, `quintIn`, `quintInOut`, `sineInOut`,
  closed-form `spring(t, {freq, damping})`, `clamp`, `lerp`, `seg(t, a, b)`, keyframes
  `kf(t, [[t0, v0, ease], …])`, `stagger`.
- `noise.js` — seeded hash and smooth 1D/2D noise for drifts.
- `type.js` — headline/sub-line builders and pure `reveal(t, t0)` / `exit(t, t1)`
  styling per word; EN + 中文 pairs.
- `paper.js` — a sheet mesh (A4 aspect, subdivided, slight curl/bend parameter, soft
  contact shadow, optional thickness edge) textured with a sheet PNG.
- `window.js` — a generic app window: rounded corners, hairline border, title bar with
  traffic lights (or a Windows-style or browser-style variant), screen texture from a
  still or a clip, soft shadow.
- `logo.js` — the mark in 3D: the rounded tile (bevelled, clear-coated warm dark), the
  two cream strokes (rounded caps), the axis L (34% cream), the blue dot (emissive,
  bloom). Strokes can draw on (0 → 1) and be posed independently of the tile.
- `floor.js` (glossy night floor with reflection / day shadow-catcher), `particles.js`
  (sparse bokeh dust), `rigs.js` (lighting presets per world), `camera.js` (dolly, orbit,
  push-in helpers with drift), `clip.js` (§5.6).

### 5.6 Clips and events
`load.clip(name)` returns `{ duration, fps, width, height, events, frameAt(t) }`, where
`frameAt` gives the texture of clip frame `round(t·fps)` clamped to range (supports a
`rate` by the caller's mapping). Scenes call `ctx.placeClip(name, {at, from, rate})` for
each use so `film.events()` can emit the clip's click/type events in film time; the
renderer writes them with the timeline cues to `build/events.json` for the score's SFX
pass.

## 6. Capture — the real app, frame-perfect (`scripts/film/capture/`)

The built app (`npm run build`, then serve `out/` statically) driven by Playwright with
system Chrome (`channel: 'chrome'`). Viewport 1440×900 CSS px, `deviceScaleFactor: 2`
(frames 2880×1800). Light colour scheme, `locale: 'en-US'`, `timezoneId: 'Asia/Hong_Kong'`.
Reuse `scripts/demo/` (content, seeding helpers, selectors, the diagram choreography)
rather than duplicating it; import from it.

### 6.1 Frame-perfect clips
Real-time screen recording stutters. Clips are captured **in virtual time**: an init
script replaces `performance.now`, `Date`, `setTimeout`/`setInterval` (positive delays),
and `requestAnimationFrame` with a virtual clock, and drives CSS transitions/animations
through the Web Animations API (each new animation is paused and its `currentTime` set
from the virtual clock; finished ones are `finish()`ed so their end events fire). Per
output frame: apply that frame's scripted input (mouse position on an eased path, key
presses, programmatic scroll via `scrollTop` — never wheel smooth-scroll), advance the
clock by 1/60 s, let React commit, screenshot. Native caret: hidden
(`caret-color: transparent`); draw a deterministic caret overlay at the selection
instead (solid while typing, 530 ms blink when idle). Pointer: the drawn arrow cursor
from `scripts/demo/flow.mjs`, moved on eased (quintInOut, slight arc) paths; a click
shows a soft ring. If the shim proves unworkable for some interaction, fall back to
slow-motion capture (`Animation.setPlaybackRate` + slowed input) retimed to 60 fps —
and report it.

Output per clip: `clips/<name>/00000.jpg …` (JPEG q≈93, 2880×1800) and
`clips/<name>.json`: `{ name, fps: 60, frames, width, height, duration, events: [{ t,
kind: 'click'|'key'|'drag-start'|'drag-end'|'toggle', x, y, label }] }` with x,y in
frame pixels. **Acceptance:** no duplicate consecutive frames while something is
moving, no frame-to-frame jump larger than the motion path implies (report per-clip
frame-diff stats).

## 7. Score — original music and sound design (`scripts/film/score/`)

Python 3 + numpy/scipy (and optionally `pedalboard` / `soundfile` in a venv at
`scripts/film/score/.venv`, created on demand from `requirements.txt`; gitignored).
48 kHz, float, stereo. Reads `timeline.mjs` data as JSON (`node
scripts/film/timeline-json.mjs`), and later `build/events.json` for UI sounds.

### 7.1 Musical brief
Modern Apple-keynote style: warm, optimistic, minimal, precise. 120 BPM, **D major**.
- Palette: felt piano / soft FM electric piano (the 3-note motif: F♯4–A4–D5, "Supply.
  Demand. Equilibrium."); warm analog pad (band-limited detuned saws, low-passed, slow
  attack, wide stereo, long reverb); glassy pluck arpeggio (16ths, filter opens across the
  intro, ping-pong delay 3/16); round sub bass (sine + gentle saturation, sidechained);
  tight modern drums (punchy kick with click, snappy clap with short room, 16th closed
  hats with velocity humanisation, a soft shaker; open hats in the final chorus); a bell
  lead for the diagram hero (8-bar singable melody); risers (filtered noise sweep up +
  pitch rise), whooshes (band-passed noise swell with a pan move), impacts (sub boom +
  noise burst + long tail), glassy ticks (short FM bell).
- Harmony (one chord per bar unless noted): intro `Dmaj9 | Bm11 | Gmaj9 | A6sus4`;
  grooves `Gmaj7 | A6 | F♯m11 | Bm9` alternating with `Dmaj9 | A/C♯ | Bm9 | Gmaj9`;
  breakdown `Gmaj9 | A6sus4`; final chorus lifts with the pad an octave up; end on
  `Dmaj9(add6)` sustained, the motif resolving to D.
- Arrangement follows §3 exactly: intro without drums; **silence on beat 4 of bar 7**
  (pre-drop breath); the drop on bar 8 (16.0 s); the breakdown bars 32–34 with the drop
  on bar 34; the final impact on bar 42; clean tail to silence by 94.0 s.
- Sound quality rules: band-limited oscillators (PolyBLEP or additive), no zipper noise
  (smooth parameter ramps), no clicks at note boundaries (short fades), sidechain pump
  on pad and bass from the kick, stereo width from detune/delay, not phase tricks that
  collapse in mono; low end mono below 120 Hz.

### 7.2 Mix and master
Stems: `music.wav` (with stems `drums`, `bass`, `harmony`, `lead`, `fx`) and `sfx.wav`
(timeline cues + UI events). Mix SFX under the music (UI clicks −24 dB relative, ticks
−18, whooshes/hits sit with the drums); the UI layer is thinned (one click per
double-click, at most 8 keys/s, no sound under a cue), and picture accents are lifted
until they clear the music in their own band. Big hits get an 8th-note pre-hit dip (≥ 8 dB
over their run-up). Master: gentle glue compression, high-pass
25 Hz, limiter; **−16 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP** (measure with ffmpeg
`ebur128`). Output `demo-media/film/audio/score.wav` (music+sfx), stems alongside, and
`demo-media/film/audio/spectrogram.png` plus a loudness report. The score records the
timeline and `events.json` it was built from; the report fails on a stale one. Details:
`scripts/film/score/README.md`.

## 8. Render and master (`scripts/film/render.mjs`, `scripts/film/film.mjs`)

- A small static server serving the repo's `scripts/film/` as `/film/` and the asset
  store as `/assets/` on a free port.
- N workers (default `min(6, cpus − 2)`), each a Chrome page on `stage/index.html`,
  each rendering a contiguous frame range: `seek(i/fps)` → CDP `Page.captureScreenshot`
  (PNG, `optimizeForSpeed`) → piped into its own ffmpeg (`image2pipe`, near-lossless
  intermediate, e.g. x264 `-qp 0` or `-crf 8`). Segments are concatenated, then encoded
  once with the audio: `libx264 -profile:v high -preset slow -crf 16 -pix_fmt yuv420p`,
  `-colorspace bt709 -color_primaries bt709 -color_trc bt709`, `-r 60`, `-g 120`,
  `aq-mode=3`, AAC 256k, `+faststart`; the final adds `aq-strength=1.1:deblock=-2,-2:
  psy-rd=1.0,0.15`, a low deadzone and no DCT decimation, so the shadow grain survives
  (about 2× the bitrate in dark scenes).
- The render never muxes a score built from another timeline or other events: it rebuilds
  a stale one (`score.mjs sfx`+`mix`, or `all`) and refuses one it cannot rebuild.
- `tools/motion-check.mjs` gates a range: stalls, jumps, dead stops, freezes and soft dips
  (a defocus over a still picture) outside planned cuts must all be 0.
- Must use the GPU (check `WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` is not SwiftShader); if headless
  cannot, run headed with the window off-screen.
- Modes: `--preview` (960×540, 30 fps, shutter 1, fast), `--from=<bar> --to=<bar>`,
  `--scene=<id>`, `--stills=<seconds step>` (PNG frames for review, with timecode in the
  file name), `--final` (1920×1080, 60 fps, shutter 5). `--format=portrait` renders the
  9:16 Reels cut ([`FILM-9x16.md`](./FILM-9x16.md)): 1080×1920 (preview 540×960), outputs
  suffixed `-9x16`, the landscape score muxed and never rebuilt (a stale one is refused).
  `npm run film:9x16` (no capture, no score), `npm run film:9x16:stills`.
- `npm run film` runs: capture (skipped if the asset store is complete, `--recapture`
  forces), events, score (`score.mjs all`, verified), render, then poster, contact sheet
  and `demo-media/film/README.md`. `npm run film -- --preview` for a quick cut.
  `FILM_OUT=<dir>` moves every output (not the asset store) elsewhere, for parallel work.
- `npm run film:doctor` (`tools/doctor.mjs`, ~40 s) exits 1 on errors: scenes tiling
  0..DURATION, cues sorted/in range/unique; every registry asset present; every
  `placeClip` window inside its clip; each text block, at the middle of its fully revealed
  span, inside its box and title-safe (and not overflowing at the 80% floor); the GPU. It
  warns on a stale score and on assets captured before a later `src/` commit
  (`manifest.json` stamps each asset's `appCommit`). Every script rejects unknown flags.
