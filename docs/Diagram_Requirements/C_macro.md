## Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

### AD-AS: short-run demand shock (cash handout, investment, tourism, monetary policy)
- Axes labelled "Price level" and "Aggregate output / Real output", with origin 0.
- Original AD0 and an upward-sloping SRAS, with the initial equilibrium and P0, Y0 marked by dashed lines.
- **AD shifts in the right direction** (AD0 to AD1, with an arrow). This is always worth 1 mark.
- **New P and Y marked**, with the direction of change (P0 to P1, Y0 to Y1). Usually 1 mark, and sometimes split into separate marks for P and Y (DSE2013/Q12, DSE2014/Q12).
- If the question asks only about output, only Y is compulsory, but models usually show P too.
- A verbal chain must go with the diagram (policy or shock, then the component C/I/G/X, then AD, then P and Y). It usually carries more marks than the diagram.

### AD-AS: supply shock
- SRAS shifts left (costs up, visa rules, disaster) or right (costs down).
- The new P and Y are marked. When AD and SRAS both shift left, only Y is determinate (DSE2017/Q12, DSE2021/Q11).

### AD-AS: long run with a vertical LRAS
- The **vertical LRAS** is drawn and labelled at Yf (it earns a mark in DSE2012/Q10).
- When demand grows but capacity does not: AD shifts right, P rises and Y stays at Yf.
- Growth (infrastructure, labour, R&D): **LRAS0 shifts right to LRAS1** plus AD shifts right, with Y1 > Y0. If the question asks about P, the size of the two shifts matters (a bigger AD shift gives a higher P, DSE2018/Q9).

### AD-AS: output gaps
- **Deflationary gap**: the equilibrium is left of LRAS. The gap is shown as a bracket or double arrow from Y to Yf on the horizontal axis. If the given figure has no LRAS, the student must add a vertical LRAS to the right of the equilibrium (DSE2021/Q11(a), DSE2023/Q7(a)).
- Label the gaps gap0 and gap1. The new gap must be visibly wider or narrower than the old one.
- **Inflationary gap**: the equilibrium is right of LRAS (Y0 > Yf).

### AD-AS: long-run self-adjustment (market forces)
- Deflationary gap: **SRAS shifts right** until AD meets SRAS on LRAS at Yf, and P falls.
- Inflationary gap: **SRAS shifts left** until the equilibrium returns to Yf, and P rises.
- AD does NOT move. Only the SRAS shift earns the diagram mark (usually 1), and the rest is verbal: factor-market excess supply or demand, then factor prices, then costs, then SRAS.
- For a supply shock followed by recovery, number the arrows 1 and 2 (SRAS left, then back) and show P returning to P0 (DSE2013/Q4).

### Money market / money demand (MCQ only in this group)
- Vertical Ms and downward-sloping Md, with the nominal interest rate on the vertical axis.
- Shifters of Md: income and transactions (right); e-payments and more frequent pay (left); stock-market risk or volatility, which moves asset demand Ma (right); expected bond-price rise or higher expected bond return (left).
- Shifters of Ms: open market purchase, discount-rate cut or bond redemption (right); bond sale, higher reserve ratio or banks holding more excess reserves (left).
- A change in the interest rate itself is a movement along Md, not a shift.
