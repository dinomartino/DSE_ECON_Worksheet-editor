## Group A — Supply & Demand core: what must be drawn

File short names used below:
- **BT-BSD** = By topic/FINAL_LQ/Basic Supply & Demand_LQ; **KP-BSD** = KP_ECON/LQ/Basic Supply & Demand
- **BT-SS** / **KP-SS** = Shortage & Surplus; **BT-GI** = Government Intervention; **BT-EL** = Elasticity
- **BT-CPS** / **KP-CPS** = Consumer/Producer Surplus; **BT-FP** / **KP-FP** = Function of Price
- **BT-RP** / **KP-RP** = Relative Price; **BT-TAX** = Tax
- MCQ files: **MC-<topic>** = By topic/FINAL_MC_clean/<topic>_MC

General KP marking pattern (from KP answer copies): diagram marks are listed under "Indicate in the diagram", typically 1 mark per element (each curve shift = 1, direction of P change = 1, direction of Q change / TR area = 1), plus separate "Verbal elaboration" marks. A diagram alone never earns the verbal marks.

### Basic Supply & Demand

#### CE1992/P1/Q2(b)(i) (5 marks) — files: BT-BSD, KP-BSD
- Q: US import tax on HK-made garments: effect on P and Q of HK-made garments in US market (separate diagrams).
- Diagram type: single-market S&D
- Must draw:
  - S shifts left S → S' (tax raises cost)
  - new equilibrium E → E'; P rises P → P'
  - Q falls Q → Q'
- Notes: KP: 3 diagram marks (S shift, P change, Q change) + 2 verbal. (KP text has typo "falls in price"; scheme image shows P rises.)

### Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

#### 1. Single-market S&D: shifts (demand / supply)
- Axes labelled (Price / Unit price vs Quantity; name the good/market in the title).
- Original curves D1, S1 and equilibrium E1 (P1, Q1) with dashed lines to both axes.
- The shifted curve labelled (D2 / S2) with an arrow showing direction; one mark per correct shift.
- New equilibrium E2 with P2 and Q2 marked and arrows P1→P2, Q1→Q2; typically 1 mark for the P change and 1 for Q (or one combined).
- Substitute/complement/derived-demand chains: draw the market that the question asks about (e.g. French cars, films, labour), and use SEPARATE diagrams when two markets are asked.
- Verbal elaboration is always marked separately (cause → which curve shifts → P/Q result).

#### 2. Simultaneous shifts ("under what condition")
- Both shifts drawn and labelled (D1→D2 and S1→S2).
- Relative magnitude made VISIBLE: the dominant shift is drawn clearly larger (e.g. "shift of D > shift of S", "leftward S shift > leftward D shift"); a mark is awarded specifically for this.
- The resulting P (or Q) direction that satisfies the condition, e.g. P rises P1→P2, or Q rises Q0→Q1 (DSE2015 Q11(d)); equal shifts give unchanged P (CE1999 Q2).
- State the condition verbally ("↑D > ↑S").

#### 3. Total revenue / total expenditure (elasticity) areas
- TR rectangles 0P1E1Q1 and 0P2E2Q2, or the gain "+" and loss "−" rectangles:
  - "+" = ΔP × new Q; "−" = ΔQ × old P.
- Correct positioning of + and − (1 mark) and correct size comparison (+ > − or − > +) (1–2 marks).
- Condition stated: elastic demand (Ed > 1: %ΔQ > %ΔP) or inelastic (Ed < 1).
- Draw D flat for elastic cases and steep for inelastic ones (inferred convention, as in the scheme figures).
- If P and Q rise together, it is a demand shift and elasticity is not the explanation (DSE2017 Q10(c)).
- Vertical supply cases (shares, land, licences): Q is unchanged and value = P × fixed Q.

#### 4. Price fixed away from equilibrium (shortage / surplus, ceiling, floor, minimum wage)
- Horizontal price line labelled (P, Pc, W, "selling price", "existing wage rate", $10, etc.), placed explicitly BELOW equilibrium (shortage) or ABOVE (surplus); this placement earns its own mark.
- Qs and Qd read off S and D at that price; gap bracketed and labelled "shortage / excess demand" or "surplus / excess supply" (labels a–b, ex dd0, etc.).
- For a change: draw the shift and a SECOND bracket (new shortage or surplus), then compare (larger / smaller / zero).
- Fixed-capacity goods (MTR, tunnels, tickets, HOS units, university places, hotel rooms, PRH, shares) use a VERTICAL supply curve, which earns its own mark.
- Quantity transacted = the short side (min of Qs, Qd); TR or wage-bill changes shown as P × ΔQ rectangles (e.g. Q1Q2 × P; area cdga).
- Minimum wage: W above We, employment read off D, G and L areas for the wage bill (depends on elasticity of labour demand). Ineffective control: W below We (the $40 < $62.4 < We ordering).
- Price ceiling efficiency: DWL triangle or trapezoid between D and S from Qs to Qe; when the ceiling is lowered, shade the INCREASE in DWL.

#### 5. Labour market S&D
- Axes: Wage rate vs Quantity of labour / Employment / Number of workers.
- Derived-demand shifts of D for labour; importation or emigration shifts S (label "S (local)" and "S' (local + imported)").
- Local employment after importation is read off S(local) at the new wage (CE1998 Q9(c)(ii)).
- Shortages or surpluses at fixed wages as in type 4.

#### 6. Per-unit tax / tariff / subsidy (incidence)
- PARALLEL shift of S: up (left) by t for a tax or tariff, down (right) by s for a subsidy; mark the vertical distance "t" or "tax per unit".
- New P1 (buyers' price) and net-of-tax price (P1 − t) or for subsidy P1 + s; new Q1.
- Buyers' / consumers' burden = (P1 − P0) × Q1; sellers' / producers' burden = (P0 − (P1 − t)) × Q1; labelled and compared.
  - Condition: the side with LOWER elasticity bears more. Draw the relative steepness of D and S to match.
- Subsidy: consumer benefit (CB) = (P0 − P1) × Q1, producer benefit (PB) = (P1 + s − P0) × Q1.
- Extreme cases: horizontal D (Ed = ∞) or vertical S (Es = 0) → buyers bear none; tax revenue rectangle shown.
- Total expenditure / market value (net of tax) areas as in type 3 (e.g. seller revenue net of tax 0Q'FR; loss in total market value net-of-tariff).

#### 7. Quota diagrams
- Original upward S; quota quantity marked on the Q-axis; new KINKED supply = original S up to the quota, then VERTICAL.
- Effective quota lies left of the free-market equilibrium. Show P rising (P1→P2) and Q falling to the quota.
- Quota change = the vertical segment moves; abolition = back to the full upward S (DSE2016 Q9).
- G/L areas when TE is asked (L > G if demand is elastic).

#### 8. Surplus (CS / PS / TSS) diagrams
- CS = area under D above price; PS = area above S below price; label them.
- Changes shown as "+" and "−" areas (e.g. price ceiling: + rectangle transferred from producers, − triangle lost).
- MC ↑ (S = MC shifts up): TSS falls by the band between MC0 and MC1 up to the new equilibrium (area abE1E0).
- For per-unit tax: CS loss = buyers' burden + triangle.

### Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

#### Single-market S&D: price control (ceiling or floor; also an administered price such as the $45 clinic fee, HSR fares or a minimum wage)
- Horizontal price line clearly **below** equilibrium (ceiling) or **above** equilibrium (floor), labelled with its value or symbol (Pc, Pb, W0 = min wage, $45, P)
- The original equilibrium P0/Q0 is labelled when the question compares before and after (DSE2014)
- Quantity transacted read off the **short side**: S for a ceiling, D for a floor. Label it (Qc, Q1, Qs = Qt) with a dropline. This mark is almost always awarded.
- The **DWL triangle** between D and S from the transacted Q to the equilibrium Q, shaded and labelled "DL"/"DWL". Almost always 1 mark.
- Optional but used in models: shortage or excess supply bracketed along the price line
- If an initial control lies on the wrong side of equilibrium, identify it as **ineffective** (Pa in DSE2014, P1 in the MCQs)
- Words that go with it: MB ≠ MC at the transacted Q (MB > MC means underproduction), so a DWL exists

#### Single-market S&D: quota
- Supply under a quota drawn as a **kinked S**: S0 up to the quota quantity, then vertical (DSE2024), or as a vertical line at the quota quantity (DSE2015)
- A higher quota means the vertical segment **shifts right** (arrows), P falls and Q rises
- A change in D (for example, growth with a normal good) is drawn as a D shift with arrows, while the transacted Q stays fixed at the quota
- DWL change shown as an **area band** (reduction WXYZ; increase as the band between D0 and D1), with Q1 → Q2 showing the efficient-quantity movement
- Label D as MB and S as MC where the efficiency comparison matters

#### Single-market S&D: per-unit subsidy (or tax) and efficiency
- Original S0 = MC and the shifted S (Ss downward for a subsidy, upward for a tax), with arrows
- The new equilibrium quantity Q1 and the old Q0
- At Q1, **label MC (on S0) and MB (on D)** to show MC > MB for a subsidy (overproduction). For a tax the gap is the other way (MCQ).
- DWL triangle between S0 and D beyond the efficient Q (MCQ: areas cid, ehif, bcgh)
- Optional: consumer benefit and producer benefit areas from the subsidy

#### Lorenz curve (MCQ only)
- Diagonal line of equality. A curve closer to it means more even distribution and a lower Gini.
- A shift toward the diagonal after policy points to more progressive tax or larger transfers. A post-tax, post-transfer curve farther from the diagonal means redistribution has worsened equality.

#### MSC/MSB externality diagram
- None of the Externalities LQs (2015–2025) in this bank require or show one. The marking schemes award words only: identify the external cost or benefit, state MSC > MSB (overproduction) or MSB > MSC (underproduction), and explain the remedy that narrows the gap (subsidy, carbon credits, or a market solution such as payment between the parties).

### Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

#### AD-AS: short-run demand shock (cash handout, investment, tourism, monetary policy)
- Axes labelled "Price level" and "Aggregate output / Real output", with origin 0.
- Original AD0 and an upward-sloping SRAS, with the initial equilibrium and P0, Y0 marked by dashed lines.
- **AD shifts in the right direction** (AD0 to AD1, with an arrow). This is always worth 1 mark.
- **New P and Y marked**, with the direction of change (P0 to P1, Y0 to Y1). Usually 1 mark, and sometimes split into separate marks for P and Y (DSE2013/Q12, DSE2014/Q12).
- If the question asks only about output, only Y is compulsory, but models usually show P too.
- A verbal chain must go with the diagram (policy or shock, then the component C/I/G/X, then AD, then P and Y). It usually carries more marks than the diagram.

#### AD-AS: supply shock
- SRAS shifts left (costs up, visa rules, disaster) or right (costs down).
- The new P and Y are marked. When AD and SRAS both shift left, only Y is determinate (DSE2017/Q12, DSE2021/Q11).

#### AD-AS: long run with a vertical LRAS
- The **vertical LRAS** is drawn and labelled at Yf (it earns a mark in DSE2012/Q10).
- When demand grows but capacity does not: AD shifts right, P rises and Y stays at Yf.
- Growth (infrastructure, labour, R&D): **LRAS0 shifts right to LRAS1** plus AD shifts right, with Y1 > Y0. If the question asks about P, the size of the two shifts matters (a bigger AD shift gives a higher P, DSE2018/Q9).

#### AD-AS: output gaps
- **Deflationary gap**: the equilibrium is left of LRAS. The gap is shown as a bracket or double arrow from Y to Yf on the horizontal axis. If the given figure has no LRAS, the student must add a vertical LRAS to the right of the equilibrium (DSE2021/Q11(a), DSE2023/Q7(a)).
- Label the gaps gap0 and gap1. The new gap must be visibly wider or narrower than the old one.
- **Inflationary gap**: the equilibrium is right of LRAS (Y0 > Yf).

#### AD-AS: long-run self-adjustment (market forces)
- Deflationary gap: **SRAS shifts right** until AD meets SRAS on LRAS at Yf, and P falls.
- Inflationary gap: **SRAS shifts left** until the equilibrium returns to Yf, and P rises.
- AD does NOT move. Only the SRAS shift earns the diagram mark (usually 1), and the rest is verbal: factor-market excess supply or demand, then factor prices, then costs, then SRAS.
- For a supply shock followed by recovery, number the arrows 1 and 2 (SRAS left, then back) and show P returning to P0 (DSE2013/Q4).

#### Money market / money demand (MCQ only in this group)
- Vertical Ms and downward-sloping Md, with the nominal interest rate on the vertical axis.
- Shifters of Md: income and transactions (right); e-payments and more frequent pay (left); stock-market risk or volatility, which moves asset demand Ma (right); expected bond-price rise or higher expected bond return (left).
- Shifters of Ms: open market purchase, discount-rate cut or bond redemption (right); bond sale, higher reserve ratio or banks holding more excess reserves (left).
- A change in the interest rate itself is a movement along Md, not a shift.

### Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

#### A. Exchange rate → import/export value on a single demand curve (11 LQs)
1. Label the price axis in the currency the question specifies (HK$, US$, Yen, JPY, domestic currency, or "Fares (in HKD)"). This decides whether the change is a movement along D or a shift of D.
2. **Price in the buyer's currency changes → movement ALONG D:**
   - arrows P1 → P2 and Q1 → Q2 (dashed guide lines to both axes); 1 mark almost always
   - two rectangles, correctly placed and labelled: GAIN/+ = ΔP × new Q; LOSS/− = old P × ΔQ; 1 mark in some schemes
   - relative size drawn to match the condition (usually 1–2 marks, the most heavily weighted diagram mark):
     - elastic (flat D): price rise gives + < −, value falls; price fall gives gain > loss, value rises
     - inelastic (steep D): price rise gives + > −, value rises
3. **Price fixed in the seller's currency** (DSE2014 Q11, DSE2023 Q9): a horizontal price line; D0 → D1 shifts right; Q0 → Q1; "+"/Gain rectangle = P × (Q1 − Q0).
4. **Substitute goods** (CE1999 Q9): D shifts left D1 → D2 with upward S; P and Q both fall; revenue area 0P1E1Q1 → 0P2E2Q2.
5. The verbal answer must state the elasticity condition and the %ΔP against %ΔQ comparison. The diagram alone typically earns only 2–3 of 6–8 marks.

#### B. Small-open-economy tariff / quota diagram (2 LQs, many MCQs)
1. Given: domestic S and D, with Pw below the autarky price.
2. Tariff:
   - a new horizontal line labelled exactly "Pw + t" (not Pw1/Pw2)
   - Q1 (domestic production) at S ∩ (Pw + t)
   - QM (imports) = the gap to D ∩ (Pw + t)
   - tariff revenue = rectangle t × QM
   - (MCQ) PS gain = the trapezium left of S; DWL = the two triangles
3. Quota:
   - kinked "S with quota" = S up to Pw, a horizontal segment of length = quota (QA) at Pw, then S shifted right by the quota
   - equilibrium (EA) where D meets the upward part above Pw
   - any demand shift drawn first (D0 → D1)
   - quota rent = the central rectangle (MCQ)
4. MCQ comparative statics:
   - under a tariff, a change in Pw moves Pw + t one-for-one
   - under a binding quota, the domestic price is set by D and (S + quota), independent of Pw

#### C. Monopoly firm: D, MR, MC (10 LQs, Elective 1)
1. MR drawn from the same vertical intercept as D and twice as steep (1 mark in nearly every question).
2. MC as specified:
   - horizontal (constant AC/MC), or
   - on the axis (MC = 0; then QM is at the midpoint of D), or
   - upward or U-shaped (given)
3. Qm (or QM, Q1, Q0) at MR = MC, and Pm read up to D (dashed lines); usually (1) + (1).
4. Efficient output QE (or Qc, Q1) where D (= MB) = MC; the competitive benchmark gives Pc and Qc.
5. Deadweight loss triangle between D and MC, from Qm to the efficient Q, shaded and labelled DL.
6. Cost shocks:
   - variable cost change: MC shifts (MC1 → MC2 or new MC), giving a new Q and P via MR = new MC, plus old and new DWL drawn separately
   - lump-sum tax: no change; label Qt = Qm, Pt = Pm, DL0 = DL1
7. "Same P and Q after MC falls" (DSE2016): shade the increase in DL between the old and new MC.

#### D. PPF / CPF trade diagrams (11 LQs, Elective 2)
1. Linear PPFs:
   - draw from the resource data, e.g. PPF_B (0,60)–(30,0); PPF (0,4)–(10,0)
   - complete specialisation at an axis intercept
2. Concave PPFs:
   - production after trade is where the world price line (TOT) is tangent to the PPF
   - it moves toward the comparative-advantage good (compare the PPF slope at the no-trade point with the TOT)
   - with no trade, the consumption point equals the production point
3. CPF:
   - a straight line with slope = TOT, through or from the post-trade production point
   - parallel to the given world price line
   - parallel for both trading partners
4. Consumption point after trade:
   - on the CPF, outside the PPF (northeast of the no-trade point, or at given coordinates such as (6,6), (10,20), (30,30))
   - often with a constraint, e.g. "same X as before" means directly above the old point
5. Export and import volumes as brackets on the axes: the horizontal gap between production and consumption for one good, the vertical gap for the other. One mark each.
6. Productivity or resource growth: shift the PPF (and CPF) outward. The direction of trade is unchanged if relative opportunity costs are unchanged.
7. Marks are almost always 1 per correctly positioned element (point, frontier or bracket), with short verbal justification (TOT = domestic MC; consumption beyond the PPF means a gain).

### Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

#### Single-market S&D: one shift (the most frequent type in S4-R3)
- Label the axes (Price / Unit price / Wage rate; Quantity), and label every curve and its new position (D1→D2, S→S') with an arrow showing the direction of the shift.
- Mark the old and new equilibria (E1, E2) with dotted lines to both axes. Label P1→P2 and Q1→Q2 with arrows showing the direction of change. The scheme typically gives 1 mark for the shift, 1 for P and 1 for Q.
- If the question asks about revenue, expenditure or market value: shade the change in TR, either as an L-shaped area (both rise, or both fall) or as separate "+" and "−" rectangles, and label it or add a legend. This is usually a separate mark.
- Related-market questions (substitutes, complements, derived demand, imported workers) need the right curve to shift. Separate markets need separate diagrams: one per market, each with its own shift.
- Verbal explanation is always needed alongside the diagram; the diagram alone gets about half the marks.

#### Two simultaneous shifts, asked as "under what condition"
- Draw both shifts (D and S), each with an arrow.
- Draw the relative size of the shifts so that it matches the stated condition (e.g. "D shift > S shift"). This is a separate mark.
- Show the final P (or Q) moving in the direction asked about.
- Say in words that the result depends on the relative size of the shifts.

#### Price fixed away from equilibrium (shortage or surplus, vertical supply)
- A horizontal line at the fixed price (P, P̄, W, tuition fee, toll), clearly above or below the equilibrium. This is always a separate mark.
- A vertical S when capacity or quantity is fixed (shares, tickets, tunnel, university places, hotel rooms, PRH flats, taxi licences).
- Excess demand or excess supply bracketed on the price line between the S and D curves, labelled (ab, ex dd0, "shortage", Qs–Qd).
- After a change: the new curve (D shifts, S shifts, or a new price line) and a NEW bracket that is visibly larger or smaller than the original. Label both, as original and new (a and b, ex dd0 and ex dd1, Ex. D1 and Ex. D2).
- Quantity transacted equals the short side (read off S when P < Pe, off D when P > Pe). Revenue or wage bill is P×(short side), shown as a shaded rectangle (capital raised, loss in wage earnings, increase in revenue).

#### Labour market (wage rate)
- Demand for labour is derived demand: it shifts with the output market.
- Importing workers or immigration: S(local) plus a separate S'(local + imported), placed to the right.
- Local employment at the new wage is read off S(local). This was a 3-mark part in CE1998.
- Fixed-wage versions follow the shortage and surplus checklist above.

#### Elasticity and total revenue (S4-R4)
- Movement along a single D for a price change, or an S shift for cost, supply or subsidy shocks. Say which it is, and do not shift D.
- Draw two rectangles: "+" is the price-change strip × the quantity that remains; "−" is the quantity-change strip × the other price. Label them "+" or "gain" and "−" or "loss".
- Draw the rectangles so their relative size matches the condition: gain > loss (or loss > gain). This is usually a separate mark.
- Make the steepness of D consistent with the claim: steep (inelastic) when a price rise increases TR, flat (elastic) when it decreases TR.
- A vertical S for a fixed licence count. A downward shift of S for a per-unit subsidy.
- When P and Q both rose (e.g. DSE2017 MTR), draw a D shift and do NOT infer inelasticity.

#### Subsidy, quota and price-control extensions (KP quiz)
- Per-unit subsidy: S shifts down by the subsidy; label the new P and Q; label the CB area (the Pold→Pnew strip) and the PB area (the strip above Pold up to the supply price); make their relative size match Ed against Es.
- Quota: a kinked S that shifts right when the quota is enlarged; new P and Q; gain against loss rectangles.
- Price ceiling and DWL: the ceiling line below Pe; the DWL triangle between MB (D) and MC (S) from Qs to the efficient Q; after D shifts left, a visibly smaller triangle.

#### MCQ graph patterns (R3-MC, R4-MC)
- Identify whether a change is a shift of the curve or a movement along it (A→B along SS means D changed; X→Y along D means S changed).
- Four-point double-shift grids: pick the point matching the direction of both shifts.
- Related-market option diagrams: identify the correct curve and shift direction in each market, including the substitute's market.
- TR and elasticity graphs: rectangular hyperbola means unitary elasticity (equal areas); the upper half of a straight-line D is elastic; compare gain and loss areas.

### Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

**Single-market S&D: surplus areas**
- CS = the triangle under D and above the price. PS = the triangle above S and below the price. Always draw the horizontal price line from the equilibrium to the axis.
- A tax on sellers: S shifts up by t (arrow labelled "tax"). Show P0 → P1 and Q0 → Q1. Split the buyers' burden and the sellers' burden around P0. The CS loss is the buyers'-burden rectangle plus the small triangle.
- A rise in MC: S = MC shifts up, E0 → E1. The TSS loss is the band between the two S curves up to D.

**S&D with price controls (ceiling, floor, minimum wage, set fares): efficiency**
- Draw the control line on the correct side of equilibrium: a ceiling or low fee below, a floor, minimum wage or high fare above. Label it (Pc, Pb, W0 = min wage, $45).
- Mark the quantity transacted on the short side: a ceiling reads from S, a floor reads from D. Label it Q1 / Qc / Qs = Qt.
- Mark the shortage or excess-supply bracket where relevant.
- Shade the DWL triangle between D and S, from the quantity transacted to Qe, and label it "DL".
- The verbal marks always pair with this: "MB > MC → underproduction → DWL".
- For a CS-change question under a ceiling: + rectangle (price fall on the units still bought) and – triangle (units no longer bought).

**S&D with a quota (domestic)**
- Supply becomes kinked: S (= MC) up to the quota, then vertical at the quota Q.
- A quota increase moves the vertical segment right (S1 → S2), P↓ Q↑, and DWL falls (area WXYZ).
- A demand increase under a fixed quota: D0 → D1, Q stays at the quota, the efficient Q rises, and DWL grows (band between D0 and D1).

**S&D with a per-unit subsidy**
- S shifts down (Ss). Mark the buyers' price P1 and the sellers' price P2 = P1 + s.
- At the new Q1, MC (on the original S0) > MB (on D). Mark both points explicitly. The overproduction DWL triangle is optional.

**Small-open-economy trade diagrams (tariff / import quota)**
- Tariff: a horizontal line labelled **Pw + t** (not "Pw1"). Q1 = domestic production on S. QM = the bracket between S and D at Pw + t. Tariff revenue = the rectangle t × QM. (MCQs also use PS gain = the trapezoid left of S, DWL = two triangles, CS loss = the whole trapezoid.)
- Import quota: a kinked supply "S with quota": S0 up to Pw, horizontal for the quota length (QA), then S0 shifted right by the quota. The new equilibrium EA is where D meets the upper segment. Demand shifts (e.g. D0 → D1 for an inferior good in a recession) must be drawn with an arrow.

**Exchange-rate questions: TR / import-value box diagrams**
- One demand curve, with the price axis in the currency asked about (HK$, US$, domestic currency).
- Show the price change and the quantity change with arrows on both axes (P1 → P2, Q1 → Q2), as a movement along D.
- Draw two rectangles labelled "gain/+" and "loss/–". Their relative size must match the elasticity condition: inelastic means gain from P↑ > loss from Q↓; elastic means the reverse.
- Mark allocation is usually 1 mark for P and Q directions plus 2 marks for the correct gain vs loss comparison, and the verbal elasticity condition is needed on top.
- **Variant:** when the price axis is in the exporter's currency and that price is fixed, draw a horizontal price line with **demand shifting right D0 → D1**. The gain in value is the rectangle P × ΔQ (DSE2014/P2/Q11(a), DSE2023/P2/Q9(b)).

**AD–AS (inferred; DSE2019/P2/Q10(b), no scheme)**
- LRAS at Yf, upward SRAS, AD0 cutting SRAS at Y0 < Yf with the deflationary gap labelled.
- AD shifts right AD0 → AD1, with P↑ and Y↑ toward Yf and the narrower gap marked.
- MCQ versions test SRAS shifting left (input costs) and LRAS shifting right (more human capital or natural resources).

**Monopoly MC–MR–AR (inferred; DSE2019/P2/Q13, no scheme)**
- Q at MR = MC, and P read up to D (AR).
- A cost fall shifts MC down: Q↑ and P↓.
- DWL is the triangle between D and MC beyond the monopoly Q, and P > MC means underproduction.

**PPF with trade (inferred; DSE2019/P2/Q14, no scheme)**
- The specialisation production point, a trade line with slope = world price, and the consumption point.
- Gain = the distance between the consumption point and the PPF. Export and import volumes are shown as brackets along the axes.

**Money market (MCQ only; DSE2026/P1/Q34)**
- Md shifts right (higher risk of other assets) and/or Ms shifts left (open-market sale). Read the new equilibrium interest rate from the intersection.

### Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

**Single-market S&D, closed economy** (IN; 2008 Q5a)
- Label the axes (Px, Qx), draw Dx sloping down and Sx sloping up.
- Drop dashed lines from the intersection to both axes and label them with the names the question gives (P_closed, Q_closed).

**S&D with world price: export/import, tariff, quota, devaluation** (IN; 2008 Q5c, e, f, g; MCQ 2004 Q25, Q26; 2009 Q25)
- Draw the world price as a horizontal dashed line: below P_closed means imports, above P_closed means exports.
- Show the import (or export) volume as the horizontal gap between D and S at that price, bracketed and labelled (IMx0).
- After the tariff, quota or devaluation, draw the new higher price line: P_open(1+τ), P_after quota, or P*open × ER1.
- Show and bracket the smaller import gap (IMx1), nested inside IMx0; for a quota, label it IMx1 = Quota.
- State the condition for zero imports: the domestic price rises to P_closed, so τ (or Δ) = P_closed/P_open - 1.
- In MCQs, name the areas: the consumer-surplus loss, the producers' gain (the trapezoid left of S), quota rent or tariff revenue (the rectangle of price rise × import quantity), and the deadweight-loss triangles.

**PPF (with IC or a trade line)** (PARTIAL/OUT; 2008 Q5b, d; MCQ 2007 Q24, 2008 Q24, 2009 Q23-24, 2010 Q24)
- Draw a concave PPF.
- Draw the autarky price as a tangent line whose slope is the relative price (P_closed), with the IC tangent at the same point.
- With trade: a world-price line tangent to the PPF at the production point (A) and to a higher IC at the consumption point (B).
- Show the import gap between the production and consumption points on the X axis.
- Terms of trade = the slope of the trade line (ratio of vertical to horizontal segments).

**Keynesian cross / leakages-injections** (OUT; 2006 Q6, 2008 Q6d, 2009 Q6; many MCQs)
- 45-degree line (E=Y), the AE line, and equilibrium Y where they cross.
- Autonomous changes are parallel shifts (G, transfers × mpc, exports); MPC or tax-rate changes are rotations.
- An auxiliary T-G line crosses zero at the budget-balance income; X-M (or X against M) crosses at trade balance.
- A kinked E line appears when the tax schedule has an exemption threshold.

**Consumption function** (OUT; 2008 Q6c; MCQ 2004 Q3, 2010 Q2)
- C against Y: the intercept is autonomous consumption and the slope is MPC (or (1-τ)MPC out of pre-tax income).
- A tax exemption up to e makes the line steeper below e and shifts it up in parallel above e.

**IS-LM** (OUT; 2006 Q3, Q5; 2009 Q4; 2010 Q3; MCQ 2010 Q11)
- Downward-sloping IS, upward-sloping LM, and equilibrium (r, Y).
- Higher desired saving or lower investment demand shifts IS down/left: r falls and Y falls.
- Higher money demand (financial turmoil) shifts LM up/left: r rises and Y falls.
- G up shifts IS right; the output effect is larger with a flatter LM, and ambiguous for IS slope (horizontal vs vertical displacement).
- In a liquidity trap, or when the central bank targets r, LM is horizontal.

**Money demand (liquidity preference)** (OUT as drawn here; the DSE money market is IN only in its simple Md/Ms form; 2009 Q4, 2010 Q3b, MCQ 2008 Q20)
- Asset demand Ma is convex and flattens into a liquidity-trap segment.
- It shifts right when a rise in r is expected, and when income rises (transaction demand).

**Conclusion for DSE use.** Only the S&D and world-price items qualify: **2008 AL P2 Q5(a), (c), (e), (f), (g)** and **MCQs 2004 Q25-26 and 2009 Q25** are fully in the current HKDSE syllabus. The PPF items are partly usable. Every other diagram question here depends on the 45-degree Keynesian cross, the consumption function, IS-LM or indifference curves, all of which the HKDSE removed.

## 8. Master checklist by diagram type (collected)

### From part: A_supply_demand


#### 1. Single-market S&D: shifts (demand / supply)
- Axes labelled (Price / Unit price vs Quantity; name the good/market in the title).
- Original curves D1, S1 and equilibrium E1 (P1, Q1) with dashed lines to both axes.
- The shifted curve labelled (D2 / S2) with an arrow showing direction; one mark per correct shift.
- New equilibrium E2 with P2 and Q2 marked and arrows P1→P2, Q1→Q2; typically 1 mark for the P change and 1 for Q (or one combined).
- Substitute/complement/derived-demand chains: draw the market that the question asks about (e.g. French cars, films, labour), and use SEPARATE diagrams when two markets are asked.
- Verbal elaboration is always marked separately (cause → which curve shifts → P/Q result).

#### 2. Simultaneous shifts ("under what condition")
- Both shifts drawn and labelled (D1→D2 and S1→S2).
- Relative magnitude made VISIBLE: the dominant shift is drawn clearly larger (e.g. "shift of D > shift of S", "leftward S shift > leftward D shift"); a mark is awarded specifically for this.
- The resulting P (or Q) direction that satisfies the condition, e.g. P rises P1→P2, or Q rises Q0→Q1 (DSE2015 Q11(d)); equal shifts give unchanged P (CE1999 Q2).
- State the condition verbally ("↑D > ↑S").

#### 3. Total revenue / total expenditure (elasticity) areas
- TR rectangles 0P1E1Q1 and 0P2E2Q2, or the gain "+" and loss "−" rectangles:
  - "+" = ΔP × new Q; "−" = ΔQ × old P.
- Correct positioning of + and − (1 mark) and correct size comparison (+ > − or − > +) (1–2 marks).
- Condition stated: elastic demand (Ed > 1: %ΔQ > %ΔP) or inelastic (Ed < 1).
- Draw D flat for elastic cases and steep for inelastic ones (inferred convention, as in the scheme figures).
- If P and Q rise together, it is a demand shift and elasticity is not the explanation (DSE2017 Q10(c)).
- Vertical supply cases (shares, land, licences): Q is unchanged and value = P × fixed Q.

#### 4. Price fixed away from equilibrium (shortage / surplus, ceiling, floor, minimum wage)
- Horizontal price line labelled (P, Pc, W, "selling price", "existing wage rate", $10, etc.), placed explicitly BELOW equilibrium (shortage) or ABOVE (surplus); this placement earns its own mark.
- Qs and Qd read off S and D at that price; gap bracketed and labelled "shortage / excess demand" or "surplus / excess supply" (labels a–b, ex dd0, etc.).
- For a change: draw the shift and a SECOND bracket (new shortage or surplus), then compare (larger / smaller / zero).
- Fixed-capacity goods (MTR, tunnels, tickets, HOS units, university places, hotel rooms, PRH, shares) use a VERTICAL supply curve, which earns its own mark.
- Quantity transacted = the short side (min of Qs, Qd); TR or wage-bill changes shown as P × ΔQ rectangles (e.g. Q1Q2 × P; area cdga).
- Minimum wage: W above We, employment read off D, G and L areas for the wage bill (depends on elasticity of labour demand). Ineffective control: W below We (the $40 < $62.4 < We ordering).
- Price ceiling efficiency: DWL triangle or trapezoid between D and S from Qs to Qe; when the ceiling is lowered, shade the INCREASE in DWL.

#### 5. Labour market S&D
- Axes: Wage rate vs Quantity of labour / Employment / Number of workers.
- Derived-demand shifts of D for labour; importation or emigration shifts S (label "S (local)" and "S' (local + imported)").
- Local employment after importation is read off S(local) at the new wage (CE1998 Q9(c)(ii)).
- Shortages or surpluses at fixed wages as in type 4.

#### 6. Per-unit tax / tariff / subsidy (incidence)
- PARALLEL shift of S: up (left) by t for a tax or tariff, down (right) by s for a subsidy; mark the vertical distance "t" or "tax per unit".
- New P1 (buyers' price) and net-of-tax price (P1 − t) or for subsidy P1 + s; new Q1.
- Buyers' / consumers' burden = (P1 − P0) × Q1; sellers' / producers' burden = (P0 − (P1 − t)) × Q1; labelled and compared.
  - Condition: the side with LOWER elasticity bears more. Draw the relative steepness of D and S to match.
- Subsidy: consumer benefit (CB) = (P0 − P1) × Q1, producer benefit (PB) = (P1 + s − P0) × Q1.
- Extreme cases: horizontal D (Ed = ∞) or vertical S (Es = 0) → buyers bear none; tax revenue rectangle shown.
- Total expenditure / market value (net of tax) areas as in type 3 (e.g. seller revenue net of tax 0Q'FR; loss in total market value net-of-tariff).

#### 7. Quota diagrams
- Original upward S; quota quantity marked on the Q-axis; new KINKED supply = original S up to the quota, then VERTICAL.
- Effective quota lies left of the free-market equilibrium. Show P rising (P1→P2) and Q falling to the quota.
- Quota change = the vertical segment moves; abolition = back to the full upward S (DSE2016 Q9).
- G/L areas when TE is asked (L > G if demand is elastic).

#### 8. Surplus (CS / PS / TSS) diagrams
- CS = area under D above price; PS = area above S below price; label them.
- Changes shown as "+" and "−" areas (e.g. price ceiling: + rectangle transferred from producers, − triangle lost).
- MC ↑ (S = MC shifts up): TSS falls by the band between MC0 and MC1 up to the new equilibrium (area abE1E0).
- For per-unit tax: CS loss = buyers' burden + triangle.

### From part: B_firms_micro


#### Single-market S&D: price control (ceiling or floor; also an administered price such as the $45 clinic fee, HSR fares or a minimum wage)
- Horizontal price line clearly **below** equilibrium (ceiling) or **above** equilibrium (floor), labelled with its value or symbol (Pc, Pb, W0 = min wage, $45, P)
- The original equilibrium P0/Q0 is labelled when the question compares before and after (DSE2014)
- Quantity transacted read off the **short side**: S for a ceiling, D for a floor. Label it (Qc, Q1, Qs = Qt) with a dropline. This mark is almost always awarded.
- The **DWL triangle** between D and S from the transacted Q to the equilibrium Q, shaded and labelled "DL"/"DWL". Almost always 1 mark.
- Optional but used in models: shortage or excess supply bracketed along the price line
- If an initial control lies on the wrong side of equilibrium, identify it as **ineffective** (Pa in DSE2014, P1 in the MCQs)
- Words that go with it: MB ≠ MC at the transacted Q (MB > MC means underproduction), so a DWL exists

#### Single-market S&D: quota
- Supply under a quota drawn as a **kinked S**: S0 up to the quota quantity, then vertical (DSE2024), or as a vertical line at the quota quantity (DSE2015)
- A higher quota means the vertical segment **shifts right** (arrows), P falls and Q rises
- A change in D (for example, growth with a normal good) is drawn as a D shift with arrows, while the transacted Q stays fixed at the quota
- DWL change shown as an **area band** (reduction WXYZ; increase as the band between D0 and D1), with Q1 → Q2 showing the efficient-quantity movement
- Label D as MB and S as MC where the efficiency comparison matters

#### Single-market S&D: per-unit subsidy (or tax) and efficiency
- Original S0 = MC and the shifted S (Ss downward for a subsidy, upward for a tax), with arrows
- The new equilibrium quantity Q1 and the old Q0
- At Q1, **label MC (on S0) and MB (on D)** to show MC > MB for a subsidy (overproduction). For a tax the gap is the other way (MCQ).
- DWL triangle between S0 and D beyond the efficient Q (MCQ: areas cid, ehif, bcgh)
- Optional: consumer benefit and producer benefit areas from the subsidy

#### Lorenz curve (MCQ only)
- Diagonal line of equality. A curve closer to it means more even distribution and a lower Gini.
- A shift toward the diagonal after policy points to more progressive tax or larger transfers. A post-tax, post-transfer curve farther from the diagonal means redistribution has worsened equality.

#### MSC/MSB externality diagram
- None of the Externalities LQs (2015–2025) in this bank require or show one. The marking schemes award words only: identify the external cost or benefit, state MSC > MSB (overproduction) or MSB > MSC (underproduction), and explain the remedy that narrows the gap (subsidy, carbon credits, or a market solution such as payment between the parties).

### From part: C_macro


#### AD-AS: short-run demand shock (cash handout, investment, tourism, monetary policy)
- Axes labelled "Price level" and "Aggregate output / Real output", with origin 0.
- Original AD0 and an upward-sloping SRAS, with the initial equilibrium and P0, Y0 marked by dashed lines.
- **AD shifts in the right direction** (AD0 to AD1, with an arrow). This is always worth 1 mark.
- **New P and Y marked**, with the direction of change (P0 to P1, Y0 to Y1). Usually 1 mark, and sometimes split into separate marks for P and Y (DSE2013/Q12, DSE2014/Q12).
- If the question asks only about output, only Y is compulsory, but models usually show P too.
- A verbal chain must go with the diagram (policy or shock, then the component C/I/G/X, then AD, then P and Y). It usually carries more marks than the diagram.

#### AD-AS: supply shock
- SRAS shifts left (costs up, visa rules, disaster) or right (costs down).
- The new P and Y are marked. When AD and SRAS both shift left, only Y is determinate (DSE2017/Q12, DSE2021/Q11).

#### AD-AS: long run with a vertical LRAS
- The **vertical LRAS** is drawn and labelled at Yf (it earns a mark in DSE2012/Q10).
- When demand grows but capacity does not: AD shifts right, P rises and Y stays at Yf.
- Growth (infrastructure, labour, R&D): **LRAS0 shifts right to LRAS1** plus AD shifts right, with Y1 > Y0. If the question asks about P, the size of the two shifts matters (a bigger AD shift gives a higher P, DSE2018/Q9).

#### AD-AS: output gaps
- **Deflationary gap**: the equilibrium is left of LRAS. The gap is shown as a bracket or double arrow from Y to Yf on the horizontal axis. If the given figure has no LRAS, the student must add a vertical LRAS to the right of the equilibrium (DSE2021/Q11(a), DSE2023/Q7(a)).
- Label the gaps gap0 and gap1. The new gap must be visibly wider or narrower than the old one.
- **Inflationary gap**: the equilibrium is right of LRAS (Y0 > Yf).

#### AD-AS: long-run self-adjustment (market forces)
- Deflationary gap: **SRAS shifts right** until AD meets SRAS on LRAS at Yf, and P falls.
- Inflationary gap: **SRAS shifts left** until the equilibrium returns to Yf, and P rises.
- AD does NOT move. Only the SRAS shift earns the diagram mark (usually 1), and the rest is verbal: factor-market excess supply or demand, then factor prices, then costs, then SRAS.
- For a supply shock followed by recovery, number the arrows 1 and 2 (SRAS left, then back) and show P returning to P0 (DSE2013/Q4).

#### Money market / money demand (MCQ only in this group)
- Vertical Ms and downward-sloping Md, with the nominal interest rate on the vertical axis.
- Shifters of Md: income and transactions (right); e-payments and more frequent pay (left); stock-market risk or volatility, which moves asset demand Ma (right); expected bond-price rise or higher expected bond return (left).
- Shifters of Ms: open market purchase, discount-rate cut or bond redemption (right); bond sale, higher reserve ratio or banks holding more excess reserves (left).
- A change in the interest rate itself is a movement along Md, not a shift.

### From part: D_international_electives


#### A. Exchange rate → import/export value on a single demand curve (11 LQs)
1. Label the price axis in the currency the question specifies (HK$, US$, Yen, JPY, domestic currency, or "Fares (in HKD)"). This decides whether the change is a movement along D or a shift of D.
2. **Price in the buyer's currency changes → movement ALONG D:**
   - arrows P1 → P2 and Q1 → Q2 (dashed guide lines to both axes); 1 mark almost always
   - two rectangles, correctly placed and labelled: GAIN/+ = ΔP × new Q; LOSS/− = old P × ΔQ; 1 mark in some schemes
   - relative size drawn to match the condition (usually 1–2 marks, the most heavily weighted diagram mark):
     - elastic (flat D): price rise gives + < −, value falls; price fall gives gain > loss, value rises
     - inelastic (steep D): price rise gives + > −, value rises
3. **Price fixed in the seller's currency** (DSE2014 Q11, DSE2023 Q9): a horizontal price line; D0 → D1 shifts right; Q0 → Q1; "+"/Gain rectangle = P × (Q1 − Q0).
4. **Substitute goods** (CE1999 Q9): D shifts left D1 → D2 with upward S; P and Q both fall; revenue area 0P1E1Q1 → 0P2E2Q2.
5. The verbal answer must state the elasticity condition and the %ΔP against %ΔQ comparison. The diagram alone typically earns only 2–3 of 6–8 marks.

#### B. Small-open-economy tariff / quota diagram (2 LQs, many MCQs)
1. Given: domestic S and D, with Pw below the autarky price.
2. Tariff:
   - a new horizontal line labelled exactly "Pw + t" (not Pw1/Pw2)
   - Q1 (domestic production) at S ∩ (Pw + t)
   - QM (imports) = the gap to D ∩ (Pw + t)
   - tariff revenue = rectangle t × QM
   - (MCQ) PS gain = the trapezium left of S; DWL = the two triangles
3. Quota:
   - kinked "S with quota" = S up to Pw, a horizontal segment of length = quota (QA) at Pw, then S shifted right by the quota
   - equilibrium (EA) where D meets the upward part above Pw
   - any demand shift drawn first (D0 → D1)
   - quota rent = the central rectangle (MCQ)
4. MCQ comparative statics:
   - under a tariff, a change in Pw moves Pw + t one-for-one
   - under a binding quota, the domestic price is set by D and (S + quota), independent of Pw

#### C. Monopoly firm: D, MR, MC (10 LQs, Elective 1)
1. MR drawn from the same vertical intercept as D and twice as steep (1 mark in nearly every question).
2. MC as specified:
   - horizontal (constant AC/MC), or
   - on the axis (MC = 0; then QM is at the midpoint of D), or
   - upward or U-shaped (given)
3. Qm (or QM, Q1, Q0) at MR = MC, and Pm read up to D (dashed lines); usually (1) + (1).
4. Efficient output QE (or Qc, Q1) where D (= MB) = MC; the competitive benchmark gives Pc and Qc.
5. Deadweight loss triangle between D and MC, from Qm to the efficient Q, shaded and labelled DL.
6. Cost shocks:
   - variable cost change: MC shifts (MC1 → MC2 or new MC), giving a new Q and P via MR = new MC, plus old and new DWL drawn separately
   - lump-sum tax: no change; label Qt = Qm, Pt = Pm, DL0 = DL1
7. "Same P and Q after MC falls" (DSE2016): shade the increase in DL between the old and new MC.

#### D. PPF / CPF trade diagrams (11 LQs, Elective 2)
1. Linear PPFs:
   - draw from the resource data, e.g. PPF_B (0,60)–(30,0); PPF (0,4)–(10,0)
   - complete specialisation at an axis intercept
2. Concave PPFs:
   - production after trade is where the world price line (TOT) is tangent to the PPF
   - it moves toward the comparative-advantage good (compare the PPF slope at the no-trade point with the TOT)
   - with no trade, the consumption point equals the production point
3. CPF:
   - a straight line with slope = TOT, through or from the post-trade production point
   - parallel to the given world price line
   - parallel for both trading partners
4. Consumption point after trade:
   - on the CPF, outside the PPF (northeast of the no-trade point, or at given coordinates such as (6,6), (10,20), (30,30))
   - often with a constraint, e.g. "same X as before" means directly above the old point
5. Export and import volumes as brackets on the axes: the horizontal gap between production and consumption for one good, the vertical gap for the other. One mark each.
6. Productivity or resource growth: shift the PPF (and CPF) outward. The direction of trade is unchanged if relative opportunity costs are unchanged.
7. Marks are almost always 1 per correctly positioned element (point, frontier or bracket), with short verbal justification (TOT = domestic MC; consumption beyond the PPF means a gain).

### From part: E_S4_rounds


#### Single-market S&D: one shift (the most frequent type in S4-R3)
- Label the axes (Price / Unit price / Wage rate; Quantity), and label every curve and its new position (D1→D2, S→S') with an arrow showing the direction of the shift.
- Mark the old and new equilibria (E1, E2) with dotted lines to both axes. Label P1→P2 and Q1→Q2 with arrows showing the direction of change. The scheme typically gives 1 mark for the shift, 1 for P and 1 for Q.
- If the question asks about revenue, expenditure or market value: shade the change in TR, either as an L-shaped area (both rise, or both fall) or as separate "+" and "−" rectangles, and label it or add a legend. This is usually a separate mark.
- Related-market questions (substitutes, complements, derived demand, imported workers) need the right curve to shift. Separate markets need separate diagrams: one per market, each with its own shift.
- Verbal explanation is always needed alongside the diagram; the diagram alone gets about half the marks.

#### Two simultaneous shifts, asked as "under what condition"
- Draw both shifts (D and S), each with an arrow.
- Draw the relative size of the shifts so that it matches the stated condition (e.g. "D shift > S shift"). This is a separate mark.
- Show the final P (or Q) moving in the direction asked about.
- Say in words that the result depends on the relative size of the shifts.

#### Price fixed away from equilibrium (shortage or surplus, vertical supply)
- A horizontal line at the fixed price (P, P̄, W, tuition fee, toll), clearly above or below the equilibrium. This is always a separate mark.
- A vertical S when capacity or quantity is fixed (shares, tickets, tunnel, university places, hotel rooms, PRH flats, taxi licences).
- Excess demand or excess supply bracketed on the price line between the S and D curves, labelled (ab, ex dd0, "shortage", Qs–Qd).
- After a change: the new curve (D shifts, S shifts, or a new price line) and a NEW bracket that is visibly larger or smaller than the original. Label both, as original and new (a and b, ex dd0 and ex dd1, Ex. D1 and Ex. D2).
- Quantity transacted equals the short side (read off S when P < Pe, off D when P > Pe). Revenue or wage bill is P×(short side), shown as a shaded rectangle (capital raised, loss in wage earnings, increase in revenue).

#### Labour market (wage rate)
- Demand for labour is derived demand: it shifts with the output market.
- Importing workers or immigration: S(local) plus a separate S'(local + imported), placed to the right.
- Local employment at the new wage is read off S(local). This was a 3-mark part in CE1998.
- Fixed-wage versions follow the shortage and surplus checklist above.

#### Elasticity and total revenue (S4-R4)
- Movement along a single D for a price change, or an S shift for cost, supply or subsidy shocks. Say which it is, and do not shift D.
- Draw two rectangles: "+" is the price-change strip × the quantity that remains; "−" is the quantity-change strip × the other price. Label them "+" or "gain" and "−" or "loss".
- Draw the rectangles so their relative size matches the condition: gain > loss (or loss > gain). This is usually a separate mark.
- Make the steepness of D consistent with the claim: steep (inelastic) when a price rise increases TR, flat (elastic) when it decreases TR.
- A vertical S for a fixed licence count. A downward shift of S for a per-unit subsidy.
- When P and Q both rose (e.g. DSE2017 MTR), draw a D shift and do NOT infer inelasticity.

#### Subsidy, quota and price-control extensions (KP quiz)
- Per-unit subsidy: S shifts down by the subsidy; label the new P and Q; label the CB area (the Pold→Pnew strip) and the PB area (the strip above Pold up to the supply price); make their relative size match Ed against Es.
- Quota: a kinked S that shifts right when the quota is enlarged; new P and Q; gain against loss rectangles.
- Price ceiling and DWL: the ceiling line below Pe; the DWL triangle between MB (D) and MC (S) from Qs to the efficient Q; after D shifts left, a visibly smaller triangle.

#### MCQ graph patterns (R3-MC, R4-MC)
- Identify whether a change is a shift of the curve or a movement along it (A→B along SS means D changed; X→Y along D means S changed).
- Four-point double-shift grids: pick the point matching the direction of both shifts.
- Related-market option diagrams: identify the correct curve and shift direction in each market, including the substitute's market.
- TR and elasticity graphs: rectangular hyperbola means unitary elasticity (equal areas); the upper half of a straight-line D is elastic; compare gain and loss areas.

### From part: F_S5_S6_rounds


**Single-market S&D: surplus areas**
- CS = the triangle under D and above the price. PS = the triangle above S and below the price. Always draw the horizontal price line from the equilibrium to the axis.
- A tax on sellers: S shifts up by t (arrow labelled "tax"). Show P0 → P1 and Q0 → Q1. Split the buyers' burden and the sellers' burden around P0. The CS loss is the buyers'-burden rectangle plus the small triangle.
- A rise in MC: S = MC shifts up, E0 → E1. The TSS loss is the band between the two S curves up to D.

**S&D with price controls (ceiling, floor, minimum wage, set fares): efficiency**
- Draw the control line on the correct side of equilibrium: a ceiling or low fee below, a floor, minimum wage or high fare above. Label it (Pc, Pb, W0 = min wage, $45).
- Mark the quantity transacted on the short side: a ceiling reads from S, a floor reads from D. Label it Q1 / Qc / Qs = Qt.
- Mark the shortage or excess-supply bracket where relevant.
- Shade the DWL triangle between D and S, from the quantity transacted to Qe, and label it "DL".
- The verbal marks always pair with this: "MB > MC → underproduction → DWL".
- For a CS-change question under a ceiling: + rectangle (price fall on the units still bought) and – triangle (units no longer bought).

**S&D with a quota (domestic)**
- Supply becomes kinked: S (= MC) up to the quota, then vertical at the quota Q.
- A quota increase moves the vertical segment right (S1 → S2), P↓ Q↑, and DWL falls (area WXYZ).
- A demand increase under a fixed quota: D0 → D1, Q stays at the quota, the efficient Q rises, and DWL grows (band between D0 and D1).

**S&D with a per-unit subsidy**
- S shifts down (Ss). Mark the buyers' price P1 and the sellers' price P2 = P1 + s.
- At the new Q1, MC (on the original S0) > MB (on D). Mark both points explicitly. The overproduction DWL triangle is optional.

**Small-open-economy trade diagrams (tariff / import quota)**
- Tariff: a horizontal line labelled **Pw + t** (not "Pw1"). Q1 = domestic production on S. QM = the bracket between S and D at Pw + t. Tariff revenue = the rectangle t × QM. (MCQs also use PS gain = the trapezoid left of S, DWL = two triangles, CS loss = the whole trapezoid.)
- Import quota: a kinked supply "S with quota": S0 up to Pw, horizontal for the quota length (QA), then S0 shifted right by the quota. The new equilibrium EA is where D meets the upper segment. Demand shifts (e.g. D0 → D1 for an inferior good in a recession) must be drawn with an arrow.

**Exchange-rate questions: TR / import-value box diagrams**
- One demand curve, with the price axis in the currency asked about (HK$, US$, domestic currency).
- Show the price change and the quantity change with arrows on both axes (P1 → P2, Q1 → Q2), as a movement along D.
- Draw two rectangles labelled "gain/+" and "loss/–". Their relative size must match the elasticity condition: inelastic means gain from P↑ > loss from Q↓; elastic means the reverse.
- Mark allocation is usually 1 mark for P and Q directions plus 2 marks for the correct gain vs loss comparison, and the verbal elasticity condition is needed on top.
- **Variant:** when the price axis is in the exporter's currency and that price is fixed, draw a horizontal price line with **demand shifting right D0 → D1**. The gain in value is the rectangle P × ΔQ (DSE2014/P2/Q11(a), DSE2023/P2/Q9(b)).

**AD–AS (inferred; DSE2019/P2/Q10(b), no scheme)**
- LRAS at Yf, upward SRAS, AD0 cutting SRAS at Y0 < Yf with the deflationary gap labelled.
- AD shifts right AD0 → AD1, with P↑ and Y↑ toward Yf and the narrower gap marked.
- MCQ versions test SRAS shifting left (input costs) and LRAS shifting right (more human capital or natural resources).

**Monopoly MC–MR–AR (inferred; DSE2019/P2/Q13, no scheme)**
- Q at MR = MC, and P read up to D (AR).
- A cost fall shifts MC down: Q↑ and P↓.
- DWL is the triangle between D and MC beyond the monopoly Q, and P > MC means underproduction.

**PPF with trade (inferred; DSE2019/P2/Q14, no scheme)**
- The specialisation production point, a trade line with slope = world price, and the consumption point.
- Gain = the distance between the consumption point and the PPF. Export and import volumes are shown as brackets along the axes.

**Money market (MCQ only; DSE2026/P1/Q34)**
- Md shifts right (higher risk of other assets) and/or Ms shifts left (open-market sale). Read the new equilibrium interest rate from the intersection.

### From part: G_ALevel


**Single-market S&D, closed economy** (IN; 2008 Q5a)
- Label the axes (Px, Qx), draw Dx sloping down and Sx sloping up.
- Drop dashed lines from the intersection to both axes and label them with the names the question gives (P_closed, Q_closed).

**S&D with world price: export/import, tariff, quota, devaluation** (IN; 2008 Q5c, e, f, g; MCQ 2004 Q25, Q26; 2009 Q25)
- Draw the world price as a horizontal dashed line: below P_closed means imports, above P_closed means exports.
- Show the import (or export) volume as the horizontal gap between D and S at that price, bracketed and labelled (IMx0).
- After the tariff, quota or devaluation, draw the new higher price line: P_open(1+τ), P_after quota, or P*open × ER1.
- Show and bracket the smaller import gap (IMx1), nested inside IMx0; for a quota, label it IMx1 = Quota.
- State the condition for zero imports: the domestic price rises to P_closed, so τ (or Δ) = P_closed/P_open - 1.
- In MCQs, name the areas: the consumer-surplus loss, the producers' gain (the trapezoid left of S), quota rent or tariff revenue (the rectangle of price rise × import quantity), and the deadweight-loss triangles.

**PPF (with IC or a trade line)** (PARTIAL/OUT; 2008 Q5b, d; MCQ 2007 Q24, 2008 Q24, 2009 Q23-24, 2010 Q24)
- Draw a concave PPF.
- Draw the autarky price as a tangent line whose slope is the relative price (P_closed), with the IC tangent at the same point.
- With trade: a world-price line tangent to the PPF at the production point (A) and to a higher IC at the consumption point (B).
- Show the import gap between the production and consumption points on the X axis.
- Terms of trade = the slope of the trade line (ratio of vertical to horizontal segments).

**Keynesian cross / leakages-injections** (OUT; 2006 Q6, 2008 Q6d, 2009 Q6; many MCQs)
- 45-degree line (E=Y), the AE line, and equilibrium Y where they cross.
- Autonomous changes are parallel shifts (G, transfers × mpc, exports); MPC or tax-rate changes are rotations.
- An auxiliary T-G line crosses zero at the budget-balance income; X-M (or X against M) crosses at trade balance.
- A kinked E line appears when the tax schedule has an exemption threshold.

**Consumption function** (OUT; 2008 Q6c; MCQ 2004 Q3, 2010 Q2)
- C against Y: the intercept is autonomous consumption and the slope is MPC (or (1-τ)MPC out of pre-tax income).
- A tax exemption up to e makes the line steeper below e and shifts it up in parallel above e.

**IS-LM** (OUT; 2006 Q3, Q5; 2009 Q4; 2010 Q3; MCQ 2010 Q11)
- Downward-sloping IS, upward-sloping LM, and equilibrium (r, Y).
- Higher desired saving or lower investment demand shifts IS down/left: r falls and Y falls.
- Higher money demand (financial turmoil) shifts LM up/left: r rises and Y falls.
- G up shifts IS right; the output effect is larger with a flatter LM, and ambiguous for IS slope (horizontal vs vertical displacement).
- In a liquidity trap, or when the central bank targets r, LM is horizontal.

**Money demand (liquidity preference)** (OUT as drawn here; the DSE money market is IN only in its simple Md/Ms form; 2009 Q4, 2010 Q3b, MCQ 2008 Q20)
- Asset demand Ma is convex and flattens into a liquidity-trap segment.
- It shifts right when a rise in r is expected, and when income rises (transaction demand).

**Conclusion for DSE use.** Only the S&D and world-price items qualify: **2008 AL P2 Q5(a), (c), (e), (f), (g)** and **MCQs 2004 Q25-26 and 2009 Q25** are fully in the current HKDSE syllabus. The PPF items are partly usable. Every other diagram question here depends on the 45-degree Keynesian cross, the consumption function, IS-LM or indifference curves, all of which the HKDSE removed.
