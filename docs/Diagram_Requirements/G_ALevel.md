## 2006 AL Paper 2 (Sections B and C with MS). File: `2006  Economics (AL) Paper 2 Section B_C with MS` (Chinese twin not needed)

The docx images are all answer-book form boxes. No marking-scheme diagram exists in this paper; the MS is text only.

### 2006AL/P2/Q3 (10 marks). Paradox of thrift in IS-LM. Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: "In the context of a closed-economy IS-LM model, explain (with the aid of a diagram) how an increase in desired saving will affect the equilibrium level of national saving, investment, and national income. Does the paradox hold?"
- Diagram type: IS-LM (r vs Y).
- Must draw:
  - Downward-sloping IS and upward-sloping LM, with initial equilibrium (r0, Y0).
  - IS shifts down/left (IS0 to IS1) after the rise in desired saving.
  - New equilibrium: Y falls (Y0 to Y1) and r falls (r0 to r1).
  - (Text, not drawn) the lower r raises I, so equilibrium S = I rises. The paradox holds for income but not for the level of saving.
- Notes: the MS footnote gives credit for the special cases of horizontal and/or vertical IS and LM. The marks are not split between diagram and text.

### 2006AL/P2/Q5(a) (4 marks), with (b) (4) and (c) (5). Financial instability in IS-LM. Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: (a) "Show how a downturn in the financial market would affect the interest rate and output in an IS-LM diagram." (b) Add the effect on firms' ability to finance investment. (c) Could and should the central bank target financial stability?
- Diagram type: IS-LM, plus the money-demand (Md) curve behind it.
- Must draw:
  - (a) Money demand curve shifts up/right, so the **LM curve shifts up/left**. Result: **r rises, y falls** (given a downward-sloping IS).
  - (b) Investment demand curve shifts down/left, so the **IS curve shifts down/left**. Combined with the LM shift: **r ambiguous, y falls significantly**.
  - (c) (Text, inferred diagram) a monetary-policy shift of LM to stabilise r, at the risk of destabilising output when the IS effect is bigger than the LM effect.
- Notes: only (a) explicitly requires the diagram.

### 2006AL/P2/Q6(a)(ii), (b)(ii) (4 + 4 marks). Expenditure-reducing vs expenditure-switching in the elementary Keynesian model. Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: "Using the elementary Keynesian model, explain the effects of [a higher income tax rate / export subsidies] on equilibrium income, consumption, national saving and the trade balance." Parts (a)(i) and (b)(i) say "without using any graphs".
- Diagram type: Keynesian cross (AE vs 45-degree line). A diagram is optional (inferred).
- Must show (MS text):
  - (a)(ii) Tax rate rises: the consumption function, and so the AE line, **shifts down**. Y, C, private saving and M all fall. Trade balance improves; national saving and public saving rise.
  - (b)(ii) Export subsidy: the net-export function, and so AE, **shifts up**. Y, C, private saving and M all rise. The trade balance still improves because MPM < 1. National saving rises; public saving rises only if the subsidy is not financed from the budget.

## 2008 AL Paper 2 (with marking). File: `2008 AL Economics Paper 2 with Marking` (the MS is entirely images: PDF pp.16-19, docx i_img008-016)

### 2008AL/P2/Q5(a) (1 mark). Closed-economy equilibrium. Syllabus: **IN**
- Q: "Show the equilibrium price and quantity of good X in a supply-demand (SS-DD) diagram. Label them P_closed and Q_closed."
- Diagram type: single-market S&D.
- Must draw:
  - Axes Px and Qx, downward-sloping Dx and upward-sloping Sx.
  - Intersection with dashed lines to **P_closed** on the price axis and **Q_closed** on the quantity axis.

### 2008AL/P2/Q5(b) (3 marks). PPF-IC autarky. Syllabus: **OUT** (indifference curves)
- Q: "Show P_closed and Q_closed in a PPF diagram. Add a set of indifference curves (IC) to show why P_closed and Q_closed are the equilibrium."
- Diagram type: PPF with indifference curves (non-X on the vertical axis, Qx on the horizontal).
- Must draw:
  - A concave PPF.
  - IC1 tangent to the PPF at the equilibrium point; a higher IC2 that cannot be reached.
  - A tangent line at that point labelled "**Slope = P_closed**", with a dashed line down to **Q_closed**.
  - (Text) at the tangency, MRT = MRS = P_closed, and production equals consumption.

### 2008AL/P2/Q5(c) (2 marks). When a country exports or imports. Syllabus: **IN**
- Q: "Using a SS-DD diagram, show when the country would choose to export good X and when it would import good X."
- Diagram type: S&D with world price lines.
- Must draw:
  - Dx and Sx as in (a).
  - **P_open2 above P_closed**: the horizontal gap between Sx and Dx at P_open2 is labelled **EXx** (exports = excess supply).
  - **P_open1 below P_closed**: the horizontal gap between Dx and Sx at P_open1 is labelled **IMx** (imports = excess demand).
  - Dashed drop-lines from each intersection to the Q axis.
- Notes: the rule stated in the answer is "export if P_open > P_closed, import if P_open < P_closed".

### 2008AL/P2/Q5(d) (2 marks). PPF-IC with trade. Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: "Using a PPF-IC diagram, show how production and consumption of good X are determined when the country imports good X."
- Must draw:
  - A world-price line ("**Slope = P_open**") tangent to the PPF at **A (production point)**.
  - The same line tangent to a higher indifference curve at **B (consumption point)**. IC1, IC2 and IC3 are shown.
  - Dashed drop-lines from A and B; the horizontal gap between them is labelled **IMx**, with consumption of X greater than production.

### 2008AL/P2/Q5(e) (3 marks). Tariff to eliminate trade deficits. Syllabus: **IN**
- Q: "Show how the country can improve its trade balance by imposing a tariff on good X. What tariff rate eliminates the trade deficit completely?"
- Diagram type: tariff diagram (S&D with world price).
- Must draw:
  - Dx and Sx; world price line **P_open** below the domestic equilibrium; imports **IMx0** = gap between Sx and Dx at P_open.
  - A higher line **P_open(1+τ)**; the smaller import gap at that price is labelled **IMx1**.
  - Both import gaps bracketed below the axis (IMx1 nested inside IMx0).
  - (Text) imports disappear when Px = P_closed, so τ = P_closed/P_open - 1.
- Notes: the diagram carries most of the marks; the tariff formula is required. No welfare areas are asked for.

### 2008AL/P2/Q5(f) (1 mark). Import quota. Syllabus: **IN**
- Q: "Show how the country can improve its trade balance by an import quota on good X."
- Diagram type: quota diagram.
- Must draw:
  - P_open with the original imports **IMx0**.
  - A higher price **P_after quota**, at which the gap between Dx and Sx equals the quota, labelled **IMx1 = Quota**.
  - (Text) the quota cuts foreign supply, so domestic buyers consume less at a higher price.

### 2008AL/P2/Q5(g) (3 marks). Devaluation to eliminate trade deficits. Syllabus: **IN** (S&D with a world price; the exchange-rate link is simple arithmetic)
- Q: "Suppose P*open is fixed. Show how the country can improve its trade balance by devaluing its currency. What devaluation rate eliminates the trade deficit completely?"
- Diagram type: S&D with the world price in domestic currency.
- Must draw:
  - P_open, with imports **IMx0**.
  - The price line rises to **P*open × (ER$*/$)1**; imports shrink to **IMx1** (both bracketed).
  - (Text) the devaluation rate Δ = P_closed/P_open - 1 wipes out the deficit, the same as the tariff rate in (e).
- Notes: the diagram is identical in shape to the tariff diagram; only the label on the higher price line differs.

### 2008AL/P2/Q6(c) (3 marks). Consumption function under the "τ-cum-e" tax scheme. Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: "Explain (preferably using a diagram) how introducing the τ-cum-e scheme would affect the consumption function relative to the pure-τ scheme (for Y ≤ e and Y > e)."
- Diagram type: consumption function (C vs Y).
- Must draw:
  - **C_pure τ**: a straight line with slope (1-τ)mpc.
  - A vertical dashed line at income **e**.
  - **C_τ-cum-e**: steeper (slope mpc) from the same intercept up to e, a kink at e, then parallel to C_pure τ and above it by τe·mpc.
  - (Text) for Y ≤ e the line rotates upward (NW); for Y > e it shifts up parallel.

### 2008AL/P2/Q6(d) (2 marks). Equilibrium income. Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: "Explain (preferably using a diagram) how the τ-cum-e scheme would affect equilibrium income relative to the pure-τ scheme."
- Diagram type: Keynesian cross, drawn as two panels.
- Must draw:
  - The 45-degree line **E=Y**; **E_pure τ** and **E_τ-cum-e** (kinked at e, above E_pure τ).
  - Panel 1 (equilibria above e): Y_pure τ < Y_τ-cum-e.
  - Panel 2 (equilibria below e): Y_pure τ < Y_τ-cum-e < e.
  - (Text) equilibrium income rises in both income ranges.

## 2009 AL Paper 2 (with marking). File: `2009 AL ASL Economics Paper 2 with Marking` (Chinese twin not needed)

### 2009AL/P2/Q4(b), (c) (5 + 3 marks). Liquidity trap and a horizontal LM curve (diagram implied). Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: (b) "Explain why, in the presence of a liquidity trap, the LM curve has a horizontal portion. Can the central bank increase money supply to stimulate output?" (c) "Derive the LM curve when the interest rate is the policy instrument, and show whether it is horizontal whether or not a liquidity trap exists." (d) Can cutting the interest rate stimulate output under a liquidity trap?
- Diagram type: money demand curve with a flat liquidity-trap segment, and IS-LM (inferred).
- Must show (MS text):
  - (b) The LM curve is flat over the trap range: a rise in transaction demand from higher Y is offset by lower asset demand at an unchanged r. A rise in Ms is absorbed by asset demand, so r, I and Y do not change.
  - (c) With r as the instrument, Ms adjusts passively and **LM is horizontal at the target r** whether or not there is a trap.
  - (d) Lowering the horizontal LM to meet IS at a higher Y is not feasible below the trap rate, because the money market would be out of equilibrium.

### 2009AL/P2/Q6(a) (8 marks). Cash transfers vs government spending vs coupons in the elementary Keynesian model (diagram optional). Syllabus: **OUT**
- Must show (MS text):
  - (i) The consumption function shifts up in parallel by mpc × ΔTr, so equilibrium Y rises.
  - (ii) With G instead, AE shifts up by ΔG = ΔTr, a bigger shift and a bigger rise in Y.
  - (iii) Coupons shift C up by ΔTr, the same as (ii), if they are spent on extra goods; the shift is smaller or zero if they buy goods people would have bought anyway.

## 2010 AL Paper 2. File: `2010 AL Economics Paper 2` (no MS in the English or Chinese docx; used the official *Marking _ Performance* PDF)

### 2010AL/P2/Q3(b) (5 marks). Fiscal policy and the slope of LM. Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: "With the aid of diagrams, explain whether the output effect of a rise in G is necessarily bigger the flatter the LM curve." Part (a) asks for the transmission mechanism verbally, WITHOUT diagrams.
- Diagram type: IS-LM, with two LM slopes and a money-demand diagram.
- Must draw (MS text plus inferred layout):
  - IS shifts right (G up) against a **flat LM** and against a **steep LM**; compare the rise in Y. It is bigger with the flatter LM.
  - Money-market panel: the G-induced rise in Y shifts **Md right/up**. The shift is horizontally smaller when the income elasticity of transaction demand is low, and vertically smaller when asset demand is highly interest-elastic.
  - Conclusion: a smaller rise in R, a smaller fall in I, and less crowding out.
- Notes: the MS says "graphically it is easy to show"; a diagram is expected.

### 2010AL/P2/Q3(c) (5 marks). Fiscal policy and the slope of IS. Syllabus: **OUT**
- Q: "With the aid of diagrams, explain whether the output effect of such a policy is necessarily bigger the steeper the IS curve."
- Must draw:
  - Steep IS vs flat IS, each shifted right by the same G.
  - The **horizontal (y) displacement of IS is smaller** when IS is steeper (a smaller multiplier effect) for a given vertical displacement.
  - The **vertical (r) displacement is bigger** when IS is steeper for a given horizontal displacement, meaning less crowding out.
  - Conclusion: the net effect is **ambiguous**.
- Notes: the examiners' report says the common error was not being clear whether IS shifts were measured horizontally or vertically.

## Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

**Single-market S&D, closed economy** (IN; 2008 Q5a)
- Label the axes (Px, Qx), draw Dx sloping down and Sx sloping up.
- Drop dashed lines from the intersection to both axes and label them with the names the question gives (P_closed, Q_closed).

**S&D with world price: export/import, tariff, quota, devaluation** (IN; 2008 Q5c, e, f, g; MCQ 2004 Q25, Q26; 2009 Q25)
- Draw the world price as a horizontal dashed line: below P_closed means imports, above P_closed means exports.
- Show the import (or export) volume as the horizontal gap between D and S at that price, bracketed and labelled (IMx0).
- After the tariff, quota or devaluation, draw the new higher price line: P_open(1+τ), P_after quota, or P*open × ER1.
- Show and bracket the smaller import gap (IMx1), nested inside IMx0; for a quota, label it IMx1 = Quota.
- State the condition for zero imports: the domestic price rises to P_closed, so τ (or Δ) = P_closed/P_open - 1.
- In MCQs, name the areas: the consumer-surplus loss, the producers' gain (the trapezoid left of S), quota rent or tariff revenue (the rectangle of price rise × import quantity), and the deadweight-loss triangles.

**PPF (with IC or a trade line)** (PARTIAL/OUT; 2008 Q5b, d; MCQ 2007 Q24, 2008 Q24, 2009 Q23-24, 2010 Q24)
- Draw a concave PPF.
- Draw the autarky price as a tangent line whose slope is the relative price (P_closed), with the IC tangent at the same point.
- With trade: a world-price line tangent to the PPF at the production point (A) and to a higher IC at the consumption point (B).
- Show the import gap between the production and consumption points on the X axis.
- Terms of trade = the slope of the trade line (ratio of vertical to horizontal segments).

**Keynesian cross / leakages-injections** (OUT; 2006 Q6, 2008 Q6d, 2009 Q6; many MCQs)
- 45-degree line (E=Y), the AE line, and equilibrium Y where they cross.
- Autonomous changes are parallel shifts (G, transfers × mpc, exports); MPC or tax-rate changes are rotations.
- An auxiliary T-G line crosses zero at the budget-balance income; X-M (or X against M) crosses at trade balance.
- A kinked E line appears when the tax schedule has an exemption threshold.

**Consumption function** (OUT; 2008 Q6c; MCQ 2004 Q3, 2010 Q2)
- C against Y: the intercept is autonomous consumption and the slope is MPC (or (1-τ)MPC out of pre-tax income).
- A tax exemption up to e makes the line steeper below e and shifts it up in parallel above e.

**IS-LM** (OUT; 2006 Q3, Q5; 2009 Q4; 2010 Q3; MCQ 2010 Q11)
- Downward-sloping IS, upward-sloping LM, and equilibrium (r, Y).
- Higher desired saving or lower investment demand shifts IS down/left: r falls and Y falls.
- Higher money demand (financial turmoil) shifts LM up/left: r rises and Y falls.
- G up shifts IS right; the output effect is larger with a flatter LM, and ambiguous for IS slope (horizontal vs vertical displacement).
- In a liquidity trap, or when the central bank targets r, LM is horizontal.

**Money demand (liquidity preference)** (OUT as drawn here; the DSE money market is IN only in its simple Md/Ms form; 2009 Q4, 2010 Q3b, MCQ 2008 Q20)
- Asset demand Ma is convex and flattens into a liquidity-trap segment.
- It shifts right when a rise in r is expected, and when income rises (transaction demand).

**Conclusion for DSE use.** Only the S&D and world-price items qualify: **2008 AL P2 Q5(a), (c), (e), (f), (g)** and **MCQs 2004 Q25-26 and 2009 Q25** are fully in the current HKDSE syllabus. The PPF items are partly usable. Every other diagram question here depends on the 45-degree Keynesian cross, the consumption function, IS-LM or indifference curves, all of which the HKDSE removed.
