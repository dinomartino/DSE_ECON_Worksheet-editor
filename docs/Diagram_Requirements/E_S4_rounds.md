## S4-R3 LQ

### CE1993/P1/Q4(b)(ii)  (4 marks) — files: R3-LQ
- Q: TV sets smuggled into China. Explain the effect on the equilibrium price of TV sets in China.
- Diagram type: single-market S&D
- Must draw:
  - S shifts right, S→S' (arrow)
  - equilibrium price falls P1→P2, marked on the price axis
  - (the model diagram also marks Q1→Q2 rising, but the scheme gives no mark for it)
- Notes: 2 marks for the diagram (S shifts right 1, price decreases 1) and 2 for the explanation (supply rises, price falls).

### CE2003/P1/Q11(d)  (6 marks) — files: R3-LQ
- Q: IT companies close down. Show the effect on the wage rate and employment of IT professionals.
- Diagram type: labour market S&D (y-axis "Wage rate")
- Must draw:
  - D shifts left, D1→D2
  - wage rate falls (P1→P2 on the wage axis)
  - employment falls, Q1→Q2
- Notes: 3 diagram marks and 3 verbal marks (demand for IT professionals falls, wage falls, employment falls).

### CE1997/P1/Q9(b)  (6 marks) — files: R3-LQ
- Q: A new railway is built. Show the effect on the total revenue of minibuses on the same route.
- Diagram type: single-market S&D with revenue areas
- Must draw:
  - D shifts left, D1→D2 (the railway is a substitute), with E1 and E2 marked
  - P1→P2 and Q1→Q2 both fall
  - TR falls: the model shades the original rectangle 0P1E1Q1 and the new, smaller rectangle 0P2E2Q2
- Notes: 3 diagram marks (D left, P falls, TR falls) and 3 verbal marks.

### CE1999/P1/Q9(b)  (7 marks) — files: R3-LQ

- Diagram type: single-market S&D with revenue areas
- Must draw:
  - D for French cars shifts left, D1→D2 (Japanese cars become cheaper and are a substitute)
  - P1→P2 and Q1→Q2 both fall; E1 and E2 marked
  - fall in TR shown as areas: old rectangle P1×Q1 against new rectangle P2×Q2 (dotted against striped shading)
- Notes: 3 diagram marks and 4 verbal marks.

### CE2000/P1/Q9(b)(i)  (6 marks) — files: R3-LQ

- Diagram type: single-market S&D
- Must draw:

  - P→P' and Q→Q' (Q'←Q) both fall
  - total revenue decreases (the scheme awards this; the model shows it only with dashed lines, so shading the smaller P'×Q' rectangle is the natural way to show it (inferred))

### CE1992/P1/Q2(b)(i) and (b)(ii)  (5 + 5 marks) — files: R3-LQ
- Q: The US puts an import tax on HK-made garments. Use SEPARATE diagrams to show (i) the HK-made garment market in the US and (ii) the US-made garment market.
- Diagram type: two single-market S&D diagrams (substitutes)
- Must draw:
  - (i) S shifts left, S→S' (tax); P→P' rises; Q→Q' falls; E→E'
  - (ii) D for US-made garments shifts right, D→D'; P→P' rises; Q→Q' rises
- Notes: 3 diagram marks in each part (shift, price, quantity) and 2 verbal marks. The scheme text for (i) says "falls in price", but that is a typo: the model diagram and the verbal points show price rising.

### CE1994/P1/Q9(b)(i) and (b)(ii)  (5 + 6 marks) — files: R3-LQ
- Q: The US puts tariffs on Japanese cars. Show (i) the price and quantity of Japanese cars in the US and (ii) the wage and employment of US car workers.
- Diagram type: (i) goods-market S&D; (ii) labour market (derived demand)
- Must draw:
  - (i) S1→S2 shifts left; P1→P2 rises; Q1→Q2 falls; E1→E2
  - (ii) D for US car workers shifts right, D1→D2; W1→W2 rises; employment L1→L2 rises; E1→E2
- Notes: (i) 2 diagram marks and 3 verbal; (ii) 3 diagram marks and 3 verbal. The chain is: tariff → US cars are a substitute, so demand for them rises → derived demand for their workers rises.

### CE1998/P1/Q9(c)(i) and (c)(ii)  (4 + 3 marks) — files: R3-LQ
- Q: Workers are imported. Show (i) the effect on the wage of restaurant workers and (ii), on the same diagram, the effect on employment of LOCAL workers.
- Diagram type: labour market with local and imported supply
- Must draw:
  - S(Local) and a second curve S'(Local + imported) to its right
  - one D curve
  - wage falls W1→W2 (the new equilibrium is D with S')
  - (ii) local employment: Q1 is read from S(Local) at W1 and Q2 from S(Local) at W2, so Q2←Q1 falls
- Notes: (i) 2 diagram marks (S right, wage falls) and 2 verbal; (ii) 3 marks, all for labelling Q1, Q2 and the fall. The key trap is to read local employment off the original local supply curve at the new wage.

### CE2008/P1/Q9(b)(ii)  (7 marks) — files: R3-LQ

## Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

### Single-market S&D: one shift (the most frequent type in S4-R3)
- Label the axes (Price / Unit price / Wage rate; Quantity), and label every curve and its new position (D1→D2, S→S') with an arrow showing the direction of the shift.
- Mark the old and new equilibria (E1, E2) with dotted lines to both axes. Label P1→P2 and Q1→Q2 with arrows showing the direction of change. The scheme typically gives 1 mark for the shift, 1 for P and 1 for Q.
- If the question asks about revenue, expenditure or market value: shade the change in TR, either as an L-shaped area (both rise, or both fall) or as separate "+" and "−" rectangles, and label it or add a legend. This is usually a separate mark.
- Related-market questions (substitutes, complements, derived demand, imported workers) need the right curve to shift. Separate markets need separate diagrams: one per market, each with its own shift.
- Verbal explanation is always needed alongside the diagram; the diagram alone gets about half the marks.

### Two simultaneous shifts, asked as "under what condition"
- Draw both shifts (D and S), each with an arrow.
- Draw the relative size of the shifts so that it matches the stated condition (e.g. "D shift > S shift"). This is a separate mark.
- Show the final P (or Q) moving in the direction asked about.
- Say in words that the result depends on the relative size of the shifts.

### Price fixed away from equilibrium (shortage or surplus, vertical supply)
- A horizontal line at the fixed price (P, P̄, W, tuition fee, toll), clearly above or below the equilibrium. This is always a separate mark.
- A vertical S when capacity or quantity is fixed (shares, tickets, tunnel, university places, hotel rooms, PRH flats, taxi licences).
- Excess demand or excess supply bracketed on the price line between the S and D curves, labelled (ab, ex dd0, "shortage", Qs–Qd).
- After a change: the new curve (D shifts, S shifts, or a new price line) and a NEW bracket that is visibly larger or smaller than the original. Label both, as original and new (a and b, ex dd0 and ex dd1, Ex. D1 and Ex. D2).
- Quantity transacted equals the short side (read off S when P < Pe, off D when P > Pe). Revenue or wage bill is P×(short side), shown as a shaded rectangle (capital raised, loss in wage earnings, increase in revenue).

### Labour market (wage rate)
- Demand for labour is derived demand: it shifts with the output market.
- Importing workers or immigration: S(local) plus a separate S'(local + imported), placed to the right.
- Local employment at the new wage is read off S(local). This was a 3-mark part in CE1998.
- Fixed-wage versions follow the shortage and surplus checklist above.

### Elasticity and total revenue (S4-R4)
- Movement along a single D for a price change, or an S shift for cost, supply or subsidy shocks. Say which it is, and do not shift D.
- Draw two rectangles: "+" is the price-change strip × the quantity that remains; "−" is the quantity-change strip × the other price. Label them "+" or "gain" and "−" or "loss".
- Draw the rectangles so their relative size matches the condition: gain > loss (or loss > gain). This is usually a separate mark.
- Make the steepness of D consistent with the claim: steep (inelastic) when a price rise increases TR, flat (elastic) when it decreases TR.
- A vertical S for a fixed licence count. A downward shift of S for a per-unit subsidy.
- When P and Q both rose (e.g. DSE2017 MTR), draw a D shift and do NOT infer inelasticity.

### Subsidy, quota and price-control extensions (KP quiz)
- Per-unit subsidy: S shifts down by the subsidy; label the new P and Q; label the CB area (the Pold→Pnew strip) and the PB area (the strip above Pold up to the supply price); make their relative size match Ed against Es.
- Quota: a kinked S that shifts right when the quota is enlarged; new P and Q; gain against loss rectangles.
- Price ceiling and DWL: the ceiling line below Pe; the DWL triangle between MB (D) and MC (S) from Qs to the efficient Q; after D shifts left, a visibly smaller triangle.

### MCQ graph patterns (R3-MC, R4-MC)
- Identify whether a change is a shift of the curve or a movement along it (A→B along SS means D changed; X→Y along D means S changed).
- Four-point double-shift grids: pick the point matching the direction of both shifts.
- Related-market option diagrams: identify the correct curve and shift direction in each market, including the substitute's market.
- TR and elasticity graphs: rectangular hyperbola means unitary elasticity (equal areas); the upper half of a straight-line D is elastic; compare gain and loss areas.
