# Group A — Supply & Demand core: what must be drawn

File short names used below:
- **BT-BSD** = By topic/FINAL_LQ/Basic Supply & Demand_LQ; **KP-BSD** = KP_ECON/LQ/Basic Supply & Demand
- **BT-SS** / **KP-SS** = Shortage & Surplus; **BT-GI** = Government Intervention; **BT-EL** = Elasticity
- **BT-CPS** / **KP-CPS** = Consumer/Producer Surplus; **BT-FP** / **KP-FP** = Function of Price
- **BT-RP** / **KP-RP** = Relative Price; **BT-TAX** = Tax
- MCQ files: **MC-<topic>** = By topic/FINAL_MC_clean/<topic>_MC

General KP marking pattern (from KP answer copies): diagram marks are listed under "Indicate in the diagram", typically 1 mark per element (each curve shift = 1, direction of P change = 1, direction of Q change / TR area = 1), plus separate "Verbal elaboration" marks. A diagram alone never earns the verbal marks.

## Summary of recurring diagram elements by diagram type

### 1. Single-market S&D: shifts (demand / supply)
- Axes labelled (Price / Unit price vs Quantity; name the good/market in the title).
- Original curves D1, S1 and equilibrium E1 (P1, Q1) with dashed lines to both axes.
- The shifted curve labelled (D2 / S2) with an arrow showing direction; one mark per correct shift.
- New equilibrium E2 with P2 and Q2 marked and arrows P1→P2, Q1→Q2; typically 1 mark for the P change and 1 for Q (or one combined).
- Substitute/complement/derived-demand chains: draw the market that the question asks about (e.g. French cars, films, labour), and use SEPARATE diagrams when two markets are asked.
- Verbal elaboration is always marked separately (cause → which curve shifts → P/Q result).

### 2. Simultaneous shifts ("under what condition")
- Both shifts drawn and labelled (D1→D2 and S1→S2).
- Relative magnitude made VISIBLE: the dominant shift is drawn clearly larger (e.g. "shift of D > shift of S", "leftward S shift > leftward D shift"); a mark is awarded specifically for this.
- The resulting P (or Q) direction that satisfies the condition, e.g. P rises P1→P2, or Q rises Q0→Q1 (DSE2015 Q11(d)); equal shifts give unchanged P (CE1999 Q2).
- State the condition verbally ("↑D > ↑S").

### 3. Total revenue / total expenditure (elasticity) areas
- TR rectangles 0P1E1Q1 and 0P2E2Q2, or the gain "+" and loss "−" rectangles:
  - "+" = ΔP × new Q; "−" = ΔQ × old P.
- Correct positioning of + and − (1 mark) and correct size comparison (+ > − or − > +) (1–2 marks).
- Condition stated: elastic demand (Ed > 1: %ΔQ > %ΔP) or inelastic (Ed < 1).
- Draw D flat for elastic cases and steep for inelastic ones (inferred convention, as in the scheme figures).
- If P and Q rise together, it is a demand shift and elasticity is not the explanation (DSE2017 Q10(c)).
- Vertical supply cases (shares, land, licences): Q is unchanged and value = P × fixed Q.

### 4. Price fixed away from equilibrium (shortage / surplus, ceiling, floor, minimum wage)
- Horizontal price line labelled (P, Pc, W, "selling price", "existing wage rate", $10, etc.), placed explicitly BELOW equilibrium (shortage) or ABOVE (surplus); this placement earns its own mark.
- Qs and Qd read off S and D at that price; gap bracketed and labelled "shortage / excess demand" or "surplus / excess supply" (labels a–b, ex dd0, etc.).
- For a change: draw the shift and a SECOND bracket (new shortage or surplus), then compare (larger / smaller / zero).
- Fixed-capacity goods (MTR, tunnels, tickets, HOS units, university places, hotel rooms, PRH, shares) use a VERTICAL supply curve, which earns its own mark.
- Quantity transacted = the short side (min of Qs, Qd); TR or wage-bill changes shown as P × ΔQ rectangles (e.g. Q1Q2 × P; area cdga).
- Minimum wage: W above We, employment read off D, G and L areas for the wage bill (depends on elasticity of labour demand). Ineffective control: W below We (the $40 < $62.4 < We ordering).
- Price ceiling efficiency: DWL triangle or trapezoid between D and S from Qs to Qe; when the ceiling is lowered, shade the INCREASE in DWL.

### 5. Labour market S&D
- Axes: Wage rate vs Quantity of labour / Employment / Number of workers.
- Derived-demand shifts of D for labour; importation or emigration shifts S (label "S (local)" and "S' (local + imported)").
- Local employment after importation is read off S(local) at the new wage (CE1998 Q9(c)(ii)).
- Shortages or surpluses at fixed wages as in type 4.

### 6. Per-unit tax / tariff / subsidy (incidence)
- PARALLEL shift of S: up (left) by t for a tax or tariff, down (right) by s for a subsidy; mark the vertical distance "t" or "tax per unit".
- New P1 (buyers' price) and net-of-tax price (P1 − t) or for subsidy P1 + s; new Q1.
- Buyers' / consumers' burden = (P1 − P0) × Q1; sellers' / producers' burden = (P0 − (P1 − t)) × Q1; labelled and compared.
  - Condition: the side with LOWER elasticity bears more. Draw the relative steepness of D and S to match.
- Subsidy: consumer benefit (CB) = (P0 − P1) × Q1, producer benefit (PB) = (P1 + s − P0) × Q1.
- Extreme cases: horizontal D (Ed = ∞) or vertical S (Es = 0) → buyers bear none; tax revenue rectangle shown.
- Total expenditure / market value (net of tax) areas as in type 3 (e.g. seller revenue net of tax 0Q'FR; loss in total market value net-of-tariff).

### 7. Quota diagrams
- Original upward S; quota quantity marked on the Q-axis; new KINKED supply = original S up to the quota, then VERTICAL.
- Effective quota lies left of the free-market equilibrium. Show P rising (P1→P2) and Q falling to the quota.
- Quota change = the vertical segment moves; abolition = back to the full upward S (DSE2016 Q9).
- G/L areas when TE is asked (L > G if demand is elastic).

### 8. Surplus (CS / PS / TSS) diagrams
- CS = area under D above price; PS = area above S below price; label them.
- Changes shown as "+" and "−" areas (e.g. price ceiling: + rectangle transferred from producers, − triangle lost).
- MC ↑ (S = MC shifts up): TSS falls by the band between MC0 and MC1 up to the new equilibrium (area abE1E0).
- For per-unit tax: CS loss = buyers' burden + triangle.
